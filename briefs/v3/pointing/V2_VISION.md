# How this video should be (lead's reasoning, 2026-11-20 06:30)

The owner rated FINAL v1 1/10: "script is shit, sound is shit ... old videos were better in story telling ... animation is
so glitchy ... arms is going somewhere ... no fun at all". He is right. This file reasons from what worked for us before
and what works for the reference channel, and sets the rules for the next version. It is the brief for the script stage.

## 1. What our best video did, and what this one did instead

| | Anti-theft (our best: 24.6k views, 91.5 % average viewed) | Pointing v1 (rejected) |
|---|---|---|
| Words | 217 in about 58 s (3.7 words/s) | 131 in 64 s (2.05 words/s overall) |
| Voices | ONE narrator, a steady, slightly amused tone | four AI voices, laughs, dialogue |
| First sentence | a person and a paradox: "She'd never even kept a borrowed paperclip. Then she read one sign... and wanted to steal." | the ending of an invented sitcom: "His bag is on that train. He is not." |
| Is it true? | every beat is a real event or a real experiment | the whole plot (tourist, suitcase, boy) is invented |
| The question | asked out loud at 15 s: "But why would a warning make an honest person want to steal?" | never asked; a character says "pointing at nothing" |
| The middle | a real experiment, two signs, two numbers | a suitcase rolls twice; 17 s with no words |
| The answer | plain words + an everyday picture ("Half this class cheated") | one lecture line at 47 s |
| The end | a punchline ("It gave them permission") and a twist (the bait wood was stolen twice) | "Bag... gone." |
| What the picture must do | one clear picture per sentence | subtle physical comedy (a foot hook, a lunge, a lift) |

The reference channel does the same thing as our anti-theft film, only faster and bigger: the narrator states a strange
fact in the first sentence, never stops talking (about 19 words by 5 s), and every sentence is a new, loud, simple
picture; characters barely act, they pose, and things happen TO them.

## 2. Why v1 failed (root causes)

1. **The script was built to pass retention rules, not to be a good story.** Four writers and three judges optimised
   word counts by 3 s, cut times and "beats". Nobody asked "is this fun? is this true? would I tell a friend?".
2. **We swapped a true story for an invented one.** The channel's promise is "one small true thing". The tourist plot is
   a sketch, and a weak one.
3. **Too few words, too many voices.** 2 words/s with long pauses leaves dead air that only great animation can fill.
   AI character voices and AI laughs sound fake; one good narrator does not.
4. **We asked the cartoon cards to do acting they cannot do.** Flat card characters with stick arms are fine as small
   figures in simple poses. Close-ups with foot hooks, lunges, lifts and long reaching arms expose every weakness:
   arms that stretch or detach, hands that become blobs, see-through figures.
5. **The camera sat too close.** Huge pale faces, no scene. Our older films and the reference keep people small or medium
   inside a rich, colourful place.
6. **The look went beige** (a white train, brown tiles, a hazy sky, a grey shirt), and nobody put a frame beside the
   reference before rendering.
7. **Sound was measured, never compared.** Music 18 dB under the voice is nearly silence; the narration is slow.
   The clown film's mix, which the owner loved, was never used as the yardstick.
8. **Gates replaced judgement.** Passing nine numeric checks was treated as "good".

## 3. The rules for the new version

**Story**
- TRUE story, told by ONE narrator, in the anti-theft shape: a person + a paradox -> the strange thing -> the question,
  asked out loud -> the real experiment with a number -> why, in plain words -> an everyday picture -> a punchline ->
  one last twist.
- About 190-215 words in 58-66 s (3.3-3.7 words/s), no gap over half a second in the first 10 s, no silent stretch
  over 1.2 s anywhere. Characters may add a few short shouts (the driver's real call), never a conversation.
- Something HAPPENS in every sentence, and at least one thing in each 10 s is surprising or funny by itself.
- Simple words. Every factual line checked (FACTS_v4.md).

**Picture**
- One clear picture per sentence, 2-3 s each; a new place or a new action every time; nothing repeats.
- Characters small or medium (a head no more than about a quarter of the frame height, except one or two chosen
  close-ups), simple poses that snap, held; arms only for a few tested gestures. The ONE gesture that must be perfect is
  the point-and-call. No limb ever crosses a face. No reaching arms from off screen. When acting would be hard, cut.
- At least three things moving in every shot (the world, not only a figure), and a camera that visibly moves.
- The vivid look from the look test: blue sky, strong golden sun, deep shadows, colourful clothes, a train with colour.

**Sound**
- The clown film's mix is the yardstick: a playful music bed that is clearly audible under the voice from the first
  second and changes with the story, punchy effects on every action, real places. Narration brisk and amused.
- No AI laughs. No AI child voice unless it is truly good.

**Process**
- Before building shots: the script next to the anti-theft script, read aloud in the head, judged for FUN by fresh
  readers. Look frames beside the reference. Then a preview for the owner, then audits against our older films and the
  reference, then fixes. No "final" before the owner has seen a preview.

## 4. The story this topic deserves (to be confirmed by the fact check and the story benchmark)

Candidate spine, reusing what is built (the modern cab, the platform and train, the lab with its button board, the New
York station with the striped board, the kitchen stove; new: one steam-engine cab for the origin, if it checks out):

1. **Cold open, a strange act as fact:** a driver alone in his cab points at a green light and shouts at it. "Every train
   driver in Japan points at nothing... and shouts at it. Dozens of times a trip."
2. **The paradox / the person:** the account that it began with ONE driver whose eyes were failing, who was afraid of
   missing a signal, so he shouted every signal to his fireman, who shouted it back (only if FACTS_v4 confirms it, with
   "the story goes" wording if needed).
3. **The turn:** a railway official heard it and, instead of stopping him, made it the rule.
4. **The question:** "But does shouting at a light do anything?"
5. **The experiment:** 1994, Japan's railway lab, ordinary people pressing buttons: doing nothing 2.38 mistakes in 100;
   pointing and calling 0.38: about six times fewer.
6. **Why:** your eyes can look at a thing while your mind is elsewhere; a finger and a shout force your arm, your mouth
   and your ears to agree with your eyes.
7. **The everyday picture:** it is why you check the stove twice when you only looked, and why saying "keys, wallet,
   phone" out loud works.
8. **Punchline:** he is not pointing at nothing. He is pointing his own brain at it.
9. **Twist:** New York copied it: conductors must point at a striped board at every stop (and, if the 2013 prank checks
   out, people stood under it with signs so the conductors had to point at them).
10. **Loop:** back to the driver's point.

The tourist and suitcase plot is dropped (a tourist may film the driver for two seconds as a sight gag).
