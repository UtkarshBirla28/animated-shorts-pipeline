## Facts (each line is checked against these; re-open the primary paper before voicing)
- Robinson, Gallus, Lee & Rogers, "The demotivating effect (and unintended message) of awards", working paper 2018,
  journal version 2021 (Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes). Lane notes:
  `C:\Users\utkar\research_notes\next_story_lane_B.md` #2.
- All 15,329 students had at least one perfect-attendance month in the autumn; letters went out in late January 2016.
- Three arms: no letter; a PROMISE letter (an award if perfect in February): no effect (0.006 days); a SURPRISE
  certificate for the autumn month: about 8.3 % more absences in February than the control group.
- Experts: 2 % of 119 teachers and 2 % of 188 school leaders predicted a drop.
- The "ahead of the other kids" reason is the authors' likely explanation (82 % of adults who imagined getting the
  award assumed they attended more than their classmates, against 27 % without it): it carries "probably".
- Dramatized inside the "Imagine" frame: the fridge, Mom's line, the snooze, "one lazy morning". Never say kids
  "stopped coming"; never say every award backfires (the youngest students may have been helped).
- Names, years and the paper go on boards / in the description / the pinned comment only, never in the voice.
