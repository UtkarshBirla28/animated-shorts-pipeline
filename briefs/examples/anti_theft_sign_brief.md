## Facts (put at top of SCRIPT.md). All checked against the original paper and testimony.
- **Source paper:** Cialdini, Demaine, Sagarin, Barrett, Rhoads & Winter (2006), "Managing social norms for persuasive impact", *Social Influence* 1(1): 3-15. PDF: https://research.fs.usda.gov/download/treesearch/45277.pdf
- **The park's real old sign:** "Your heritage is being vandalized every day by theft losses of petrified wood of 14 tons a year, mostly a small piece at a time." The park estimated more than a ton stolen per month.
- **The fiancée story** (Cialdini, US House Science subcommittee testimony, 25 Sept 2007, and Cialdini 2003): a graduate student of Cialdini's visited with his fiancée, "the most honest person he had ever known, someone who had never taken a paperclip or rubber band without returning it". After reading the sign she whispered, "We'd better get ours now." Do NOT give the couple names, and do NOT show her actually taking a rock; the source never says she did.
- **The experiment:** 2,655 visitors over 5 weekends at 3 trails (Jasper, Long Logs, Crystal). 3-foot square signs at the start of each path, rotated between trails. 20 marked pieces of wood were placed along each path for each 2-hour block, then counted and replaced.
- **Sign A** (injunctive): "Please don't remove the petrified wood from the park." Picture: ONE visitor taking wood, with a red circle-and-bar over his hand. Result: **1.67% stolen**.
- **Sign B** (descriptive): "Many past visitors have removed the petrified wood from the park, changing the state of the Petrified Forest." Picture: THREE visitors taking wood. Result: **7.92% stolen**, about 4.7 times Sign A.
- **The bait wood:** it came from the park's "conscience pile". These were pieces stolen by visitors who later "sent them back by mail, often with profuse apologies". The park couldn't put them back, because random placement would spoil the natural, scientifically important layout. Source: the paper's footnote 1, plus Smithsonian: https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/one-more-weird-aspect-americas-strangest-national-park-conscience-pile-180961377/
- **The trees** are over 200 million years old (Late Triassic). Re-check the exact figure on the park's page before narrating anything more precise.

## Script (voice-over, 217 words)
```
She'd never even kept a borrowed paperclip. Then she read one sign... and wanted to steal.

Arizona's Petrified Forest. Trees over 200 million years old, turned into rainbow-colored stone. Visitors kept pocketing little pieces. So the park put up a warning:

"Your heritage is being vandalized every day... fourteen tons a year, mostly a small piece at a time."

She read it, leaned over to her fiancé, and whispered: "We'd better get ours now."

But why would a warning make an honest person want to steal?

Her fiancé's professor, psychologist Robert Cialdini, tested it. His team hid marked wood along the trails, and kept swapping the signs.

One said: "Please don't remove the petrified wood." Another said: "Many past visitors have removed the petrified wood."

With "please don't," fewer than 2% of the pieces vanished. With "many past visitors"... almost 8%. Nearly five times more.

You see, we don't copy rules. We copy each other. "Many people steal" doesn't sound like a warning. It sounds like what everyone does.

It's like a teacher announcing, "Half this class cheated." The other half hears: everybody cheats.

The sign didn't stop thieves. It gave them permission.

And the bait wood? It came from the park's "conscience pile": pieces people stole, then mailed back with apology letters. So some of it got stolen twice.
```
