## Facts (all checked unless marked)
- **Parcel Post:** the US Post Office started Parcel Post on 1 Jan 1913, with postage set by weight. The weight limit later rose to 50 lb. [Smithsonian] [History.com]
- **May:** Charlotte May Pierstorff was mailed on 19 Feb 1914 from Grangeville to Lewiston, Idaho, about 73 miles, to visit her grandmother. She weighed about 48.5 lb, just under the 50-lb limit. The 53 cents of parcel-post stamps were attached to her coat. She rode in the train's mail compartment, and mail clerk Leonard Mochel delivered her. [Wikipedia] [Smithsonian] [MyHeritage]
  - Her age is given as 4 or 5; she was born 12 May 1908, so she was 5. The script says "five-year-old".
  - Mailing her was reportedly cheaper than a train ticket. That's hedged as "reportedly", so the script says "cheaper than a train ticket" ONLY if you're happy with "reportedly"; otherwise use cut-list item 1.
- **The Ohio baby:** in Jan 1913, an Ohio couple mailed their baby boy (about 10¾ lb, under the then 11-lb limit) about a mile to his grandmother. It cost 15 cents in stamps, and they insured him for $50. The rural carrier Vernon Little delivered him. [Smithsonian] [Guinness] Name spellings conflict (Beagle/Beauge), so the script names no one.
- **The ban:** in 1914 the Post Office ruled that children could not be sent as parcel post. There were still cases afterwards, and sources date the formal end to 13 June 1920. [Euronews] [Yahoo fact-check] The script says only "The Post Office banned it. Some people kept trying anyway."
- **The bank (CHECK BEFORE SCRIPTING):** in 1916, the Bank of Vernal in Utah had its brick front mailed by parcel post from Salt Lake City: about 80,000 bricks in 50-lb packages. Afterwards the Post Office limited how much one sender could mail per day. The building is reportedly still standing. ## Script (about 215 words)
```
In 1914, a five-year-old girl was mailed to her grandma. Postage: 53 cents.

Her name was May. She lived in Grangeville, Idaho. Grandma lived 73 miles away.

And a train ticket? Expensive.

But America had just started something new: Parcel Post. Mail any package... up to 50 pounds.

May weighed 48 and a half.

So her parents pinned the stamps to her coat... and took her to the post office.

Wait... could you really mail a child?

She rode the train in the mail car, between the letters and the boxes. And a mail clerk walked her right to Grandma's door.

She wasn't even the first. A year earlier, an Ohio couple mailed their baby boy a mile down the road. Fifteen cents. Insured for 50 dollars.

You see, the rules listed what you couldn't mail. Nobody thought to write "children." And in small towns, the mailman wasn't a stranger. He was the neighbor everyone trusted.

It's like a sign that says "No dogs allowed"... so someone walks in with a pig.

The Post Office banned it. Some people kept trying anyway.

Then, in 1916, Utah got the strangest package of all: a whole bank. 80,000 bricks. In 50-pound boxes.

The Post Office changed the rules again.

May made it to Grandma's just fine. So did the bank.
```
