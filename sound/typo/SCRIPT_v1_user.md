## Facts (top of SCRIPT.md)
- **8 December 2005:** J-Com (a recruiting company) listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange's Mothers market for start-ups. [AJ] [JT-1209]
- **The order:** a Mizuho Securities trader meant to sell 1 share at ¥610,000. He entered an order to sell 610,000 shares at ¥1. [Mizuho PR] [AJ]
- **The scale:** J-Com had only about 14,500 shares in existence, so the order was about 41-42 times the whole company. [AJ] [GM]
- **Cancel failed:** Mizuho tried to cancel, but the TSE's system did not accept the cancellation. The TSE later said its own system was at fault. [phys.org] [JT-1209]
- **IMPORTANT:** the shares did NOT trade at ¥1. Japan's daily price limit stopped the price at the day's floor (¥572,000), and trades happened there. So never say "sold for one yen". Say "an order to sell for one yen", and say "the price crashed to the floor".
- **Buyers jumped in:** Mizuho bought back about 510,000 shares. About 96,236 shares that did not exist were left in buyers' hands. The clearing house settled those in cash at ¥912,000 per share. [Mizuho PR / JT Weekly]
- **The loss:** about ¥40.5 billion, per Mizuho ("about 40 billion" in most reports; the early estimate was ¥27 billion). [GM] [JT 2006]
- **The stranger:** Takashi Kotegawa, 27, unemployed, from Chiba, trading online, reported a profit of more than ¥2 billion from the error in a filing. It was the biggest individual windfall reported. [Globe and Mail] The script does NOT name him or say "pyjamas"/"bedroom"; it says only "a 27-year-old with an online account".
- **The court:** on 4 Dec 2009 the Tokyo District Court ruled the TSE was grossly negligent for leaving a defective system in place, and ordered it to pay about ¥10.7 billion. The Tokyo High Court upheld this on 24 July 2013, and found Mizuho bore at least 30% of the blame. [JT 2009] [JT 2013] [Finextra]
- **Regulator:** the FSA gave Mizuho Securities a business improvement order. [JT-1223]
- **Not used (unverified):** the claim that a warning pop-up was ignored, the exact number of cancel attempts, and how many minutes the stranger's profit took.

## Script (203 words)
```
In 2005, a bank in Tokyo sold shares that didn't even exist... because of one typo.

December 8th. A small company called J-Com starts trading on the Tokyo Stock Exchange.

It has only about 14,500 shares. Each one costs around 600,000 yen.

At Mizuho Securities, a trader gets a simple job: sell one share, for 610,000 yen.

He types... 610,000 shares... for one yen.

Easy mistake.

That's 41 times more shares than the whole company has.

But how do you sell something that doesn't exist?

"Cancel it!"

They press cancel. Nothing.

Again. Nothing.

The price crashes to the floor. And all over Japan, buyers grab the cheap shares.

One 27-year-old with an online account makes over 2 billion yen.

You see, a stock exchange is like a giant notice board. You pin up "selling", and it doesn't stop to check if you really have it.

And the cancel button? The exchange's own system had a bug.

Mizuho bought most shares back. But over 96,000 of them never existed, so it paid cash instead.

The bill: about 40 billion yen.

Years later, a court ruled the exchange had to pay part of it.

The typo took one second. The fight over it took eight years.
```
