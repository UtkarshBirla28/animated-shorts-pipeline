## The quiz (binding)
- Frame 0 is a thumbnail: the clown big and bright mid-ride, a phone person close, people crossing, the money branch
  in the upper part of the frame. The horn honks and the first word lands in the first 0.2 s.
- The count shot is ONE continuous moving shot (no cut while counting): the camera glides with the clown as he rides
  through the square, with a slow push and drift; walkers cross in depth (near, mid, far). Exactly FIVE people talking on
  a phone held to the ear (bright, contrasting phones drawn in front of the head, each clearly countable at phone size
  for >= 1.5 s); decoys: headphone listeners, a pair, people alone, the clown honking at people. Nobody else holds
  anything near an ear. Nobody looks at a phone screen.
- The money: three cartoon $1 bills pegged to a branch that hangs over the path. Visible (whole, on clean sky) for at
  least 3 s of the count shot; TWO of the phone people dip their heads under it without looking (the clown is doing
  something funny elsewhere at those moments). Nothing points at it: no sound, glance, highlight or camera move.
- "How many? Five.": a counter chip shows "?" during the count and flips to a big "5"; "You saw him, right?": the clown
  rides up to the lens, waves (real wave, eyes open), honks.
- The ending: "You counted five phones." = a quick replay of the five phones with numbers 1-5 popping; "You saw the
  clown." = the clown waving; "Did you see the money?" = the picture rewinds (tape-rewind look, ~1 s) back through the
  count to the moments the two phone people ducked: freeze, a gold ring round the bills right above their heads; then
  it rewinds on to frame 0's framing (bills in view, ring fading) and the video loops into frame 0.

## Fact check of every line (sources: research_notes/clown_playalong_and_competition.md Part 4)
- The quiz scene is our own cartoon scene, not a claim about a study participant.
- "In a real experiment, most people on a phone call never saw him.": Hyman et al. 2010: 75 % of phone users said no
  to the direct question (6 of 24 said yes); Western Washington University, the central plaza, one hour, spring.
- "Ira Hyman ... needed a distraction": press quotes ("I was trying to think about what kind of distraction we could
  put out there"). Shown as a generic friendly cartoon professor, not a likeness.
- Student lines: a dramatized paraphrase of Hyman's reported anecdote ("I talked to this student who had a unicycle ...
  He said, 'What's more, I own a clown suit.'"; via a student paper quoting the NYT blog). Not presented as verbatim.
- "for one hour, the clown rode circles in the middle of campus": "a single 1-hour session", he "unicycled around the
  large sculpture in Red Square" (the central plaza). Purple and yellow suit, large shoes, red nose (shown).
- "asked people leaving: Did you see anything unusual?": the paper's open question, asked at the exits.
- The three answers: dramatized examples of the groups (pairs, music, phone). (Fact behind "what clown?": only 2 of 24
  phone users mentioned the clown to the open question.)
- "Even asked straight out, only one in four people on a call had seen him.": 25 % to the direct question.
- "Many spun around to look.": "many turned around at that point to see what they had missed".
- "people listening to music noticed him": "individuals with music players noticed the clown" (61 %).
- "a call pulls your mind toward someone who isn't there": the authors' explanation (talking to an absent person,
  picturing them in the mind's eye).
- Money tree (Hyman, Sarb & Wise-Swanson 2014): three $1 bills clipped to a branch bent over a path at head height;
  everyone had to move their heads; only 12 of 396 walked into it; "noticed" = stopped to look or took a bill: phone
  users (talking or texting) 6.35 % (4 of 63), others 19.8 %.
- "Their bodies dodged it. Their minds never saw it.": the paper's conclusion ("people can walk past a tree, move to
  avoid a branch, and fail to become aware of money hanging directly in front of their faces"; behaviour guided
  without awareness).
- Boards may show "ON A PHONE: 6 in 100" and "NO PHONE: 20 in 100", "1 in 4". Never "three-dollar bill", never "texting"
  for the clown study, never a year for the ride, never "they bumped into things", never a statistic about our viewers,
  never call the viewer a failure.
