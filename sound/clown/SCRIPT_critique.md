### 2. Comedy / voice writer

- Much of v3 read like an article ("wanted to know what people miss while walking", "It wasn't the gadget", "the team tried again"). v4 leads with things you can see and uses plain verbs: needed, owned, rode, stopped, spun, ducked, kept walking.
- Average sentence: 6.1 words. Longest: 15. One idea each.
- Quotable lines:
  1. **Unicycle and clown suit:** "A student of his owned a unicycle. / And a clown suit. / You don't waste a student like that." The topper is the narrator's version of what Hyman told the press ("You don't have a student who unicycles in a clown suit every day, so you have to take advantage of these things").
  2. **Invisible:** "But to one group, a purple and yellow clown was invisible." The colours do the comedy; no simile.
  3. **Ducking:** "They ducked under the money and kept walking."
  4. **The pair** (same shape as "We don't copy rules. We copy each other."): **"Your eyes stay on the path. Your mind takes the call."** It is also the reason, and it sets up the money result (the body steers, the mind is elsewhere).
  5. Second pair, free: "Nearly everyone ducked. Few stopped for the cash."
- Removed as smug or preachy: "Good.", "Not you, though. Right?", "Sound familiar?". The viewer gets a win ("You saw him. Easy.") and is never told they failed.
- But/So count: So x4, But x1. Below the rulebook's 8. More would be padding at this length.
- Read-aloud flags for the voice session (take best of N on these):
  - Line 31 "since the first second": s-s-st-s run. If every take hisses, use "from the very first second".
  - Line 15 "stopped people leaving": two hard stops in a row (pt-p). Fallback, 13 words: "As people left, the team had a question: did you see the clown?"
  - Line 25 "Three dollar bills": stress THREE, then "dollar bills" as one unit, so it is not heard as a three-dollar bill. Caption: "THREE $1 BILLS".
  - Line 4 "psychology professor": fine at speed, but it is the first cut if a word must go.
  - Line 16 "People in pairs": light p-p-p. Acceptable.
  - Lines 9-14 are six short fragments. Record them as one take so the rhythm is a volley, not six separate reads.

Last two lines:
- **Pick:** "You saw the clown. / [beat] Did you see the money?" Same verb twice, the viewer gets the win first, and it echoes the researchers' own question at line 15.
