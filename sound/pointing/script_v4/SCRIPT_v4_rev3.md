# POINTING v4, revision 3 (lead, 2026-11-20): one true story, one narrator

History: the draft was ranked last of six by three readers (fun 5/10). Revision 2 was ranked 5th-6th of six by four
fresh readers (fun 6/10): all four chose the time-order opening and the "dead sexy" sign, and asked for the stove to
shrink to one line, the footnote "The pointing came later" to go, plainer lab wording, and an ending tied back to
Hori's boss. The fact-checker asked for "onto something" instead of "right", a question for the stove, and black
engines. This revision does all of that.

**The spine in one sentence:** a boss copied a trick from a driver with bad eyes and made it a rule; ninety years
later a lab set a trap and found the boss had been onto something, by accident.

## FACTS (each line rests on FACTS_v4.md; nothing else is claimed)

| line | rests on | wording rule kept |
|---|---|---|
| 1-7 | section 1 | told as a story: the hedge "that's the story" comes in the very next sentence (lead's decision: readers reject a hedge inside the first sentence); "eyes had gone bad" (never blind, never "couldn't see", no squint, no disease); "a driver named Hori"; he checked signals out loud with his fireman, who confirmed; the boss did not know about his eyes and made it "a rule" (one railway office; 1913 manual) |
| 8-9 | sections 1, 4 | "over a century later"; "shout their signals" (not "every signal"); pointing added later (1920s), not by Hori; the call names the signal's meaning (clear), not a colour |
| 11-16 | section 2.2 | 1994, Japan's Railway Technical Research Institute; press unless red; 100 tries never red, try 101 red; 5 of the 12 who did not point pressed; 3 of those 5 pressed again on try 111 (ten tries later); 0 of the 12 who pointed and called. Never "train drivers" (24 staff aged 21-31, men and women) |
| 17 | section 3 | the authors' own explanation: the extra action puts a time lag between seeing and reacting and holds back habitual responses |
| 18-19 | a question and a tip | no cause claimed, no numbers |
| 20 | sections 1 + 2.2 | "onto something", not "right": the boss's rule was the shout; the lab tested pointing and calling together |
| 21 | section 5 | New York, since 1996, copied from Japan; conductors point (they do not call) at the striped board before opening the doors |
| 22-24 | section 6 | 2013, Yosef Lerner and Rose Sacktor (one man, one woman); the sign text is real |

Picture notes from the fact-checker: Japanese steam engines were black; the 1910 signal is a semaphore (arm level =
stop, arm lowered about 45 degrees = clear), no colour lamp by day; show Hori's bad eyes with a pushed-forward head
and a blurry view, never slit eyes; keep soot to light smudges; when a signal is shown from the track, show its lit
face; nothing buzzed on a wrong press in the lab (use red X's); six of the 24 lab people were women; in New York the
doors open only after the point; no steam vents on a subway platform; Japanese commuters are used to the pointing
(the puzzled one is a tourist).
