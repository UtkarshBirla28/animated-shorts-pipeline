# Read 5: comedy reader

## Ranking (the one I'd most want to watch first)
1. **Tree / target fixation (Ricks).** Every line raises the stakes: the flashing arrow, the tow-truck guy in a lawn chair, the sheriff hitting it at 15 mph, then the stump. Five laughs, with the explanation saved for the end.
2. **Rocket cart (Ricks).** "Are we fighting them or grating cheese?" and the human umbrella. It's all mystery and no lecture, and the last line lands.
3. **Pink cell (Ricks).** "Barbie's gonna love it in here." The arm-wrestle is a real "wait, what", and the reversal at the end is earned.
4. **Anti-theft (ours).** A clean hook, one great whisper, and "stolen twice" is a perfect button. Fewer jokes than Ricks, but a tight shape.
5. **B.** The strongest opening of ours. It has the most laughs of A, B and the clown, and a real loop. The play-along has a hole (see below).
6. **Clown.** The same play-along idea, with a better trap payoff ("did you see the money?"). The middle is a run of percentages read out to me.
7. **A.** A decent first line, then 30 seconds of setup before the best part, the lab test. I'd be gone by "Legend says his name was Hori."

## A vs B
**First 3 seconds.** B wins easily. "Tap your foot every time this light turns green" is an order, and an order beats a fact. A's "eyes had gone bad" is interesting but passive.

**Would I play along with B?** Half of me would. I'm on a bus, so I won't tap my foot, but I'd tap my thumb. **What goes wrong: five greens isn't a habit.** I watch for red the whole time, I don't tap, and I feel smug instead of caught. Worse, the narrator says "Go" on every green. If he goes quiet on red, the silence gives it away. If he says "Go" on red, it's a cheat and the comments will say so. Fix: no words, beeps only. Seven greens that speed up, then red lands **on the same beep**. The beep is the trap, and it's fair. Also have the "Did you tap?" line cover the viewers who didn't fall for it.

**Where I'd swipe.** A: at A3 (the "legend" plus a name I don't care about), and again at A7–A9, the "somebody added a point" detour. B: at B11, about 35 s in, when "Japan's train drivers have done it for a century" lands flat after the lab peak. B4 is also a lot to take in at once.

**Laughs and "no way" moments.** Both have the same set: cereal in the fridge, the coal man's grin, the boss who never knew, "dead sexy", "had to point". B gets one extra beat because the viewer is in on the trap. A has more "no way" in its ending (the bad eyes as the final reveal). B uses that reveal in the middle, where it plays as a laugh.

**Ending.** B. "Now try the lights again. This time... point." loops back to frame 0 and dares me to replay it. I'd type: *"I didn't tap, I'm built different"* or *"pointed at my phone like an idiot."* A's ending is neat, but it's a callback, not a dare.

## Winner: B
B starts with me and ends with me, and it puts the best material (the trap) at second zero instead of second 30. A tells the same jokes in a weaker order.

## Must-fix in B
- **"Go. Go. Go. Go. Go..."**: the spoken cue gives away the red or cheats. **Replace with:** no narration. Seven green beeps that speed up, and red arrives on the eighth beep at the same pitch.
- **"Did you tap?"**: it leaves the non-tappers smug. **Replace with:** "Did you tap? She did."
- **"In 1994, Japan's railway lab set this trap for real. A hundred lights in a row: no red. Then... red."**: too much in one line. **Replace with:** "Japan's railway lab did this for real. Not five greens. A hundred. Then... red."
- **"Japan's train drivers have done it for a century: every signal, they point... and shout."**: this is the swipe point and it needs a kick. **Replace with:** "That's why Japan's train drivers point at every single signal. And yell at it."
- **"And the story goes, the shouting began with a steam-train driver whose eyes had gone bad."**: clunky, and the twist gets lost. **Replace with:** "And the yelling? Legend says it started with a driver who couldn't see."
- **"He never knew about the eyes."**: right idea, but it should hit harder. **Replace with:** "Nobody told him the driver was half blind."
