## Ranking, the one I most want to watch first

1. **How One Cart Stopped an Entire Army.** "He drilled 82 holes into the wood" and I have no clue why. Every line adds
   a weirder thing (paper tubes, strings, a rope nobody can touch, he lies on it in the rain). I'm not learning
   anything, I'm waiting to find out what it is. "Are we fighting them or grating cheese?" got me. The ending pays off
   the very first number: 82. Zero lecture.
2. **The Instinct That Makes You Crash.** A tree on an empty road that people keep hitting. The tow truck guy with a
   lawn chair, the sheriff hitting it at 15 mph, then hitting the STUMP. Two real laughs. The "why" is 15 seconds at
   the end and by then I'd sit through anything.
3. **The Pink Prison Cell.** "Barbie's gonna love it in here" is a good laugh, the arm-wrestle is a "wait, what", and
   the twist (it flips after 15 minutes) is new to me. A bit of a wall of words in the middle, but it keeps moving.
4. **Anti-theft sign.** Best hook of all six: an honest person reads one sign and wants to steal. "We'd better get ours
   now" is funny because it's real. Middle is a little school-ish (two signs, two percents), but "So some of it got
   stolen twice" is a great last line. I'd send this one.
5. **Clown.** The count-the-phones quiz makes me do something, and "Did you see the money?" makes me scrub back. Good
   rewatch bait. But the middle is a list of reveals and the student/unicycle bit is the only story in it.
6. **Pointing v4 (the new one).** Has one real laugh (the boss and the squint) and one good "they did it AGAIN", but
   the hook is a thing I've already seen on TikTok, it's three short stories stapled together (Hori, a lab, New York),
   and there's a seven-second lecture right where I'm deciding whether to stay. It's not bad. It's the one I'd most
   likely swipe on.

Honest gap between 5 and 6: small. Gap between 6 and the top three: big. The top three follow one guy doing one weird
thing that gets weirder. This one changes main character twice and Hori, the funniest guy in it, is gone at 24 s.

## The new script, line by line (what's in my head while it plays)

**1. "Alone at the front of a train, he points at a light... and shouts at it."** (0-4.6 s)
Picture is nice. But I KNOW this. The Japanese-train-guy-pointing clip has been on my feed like ten times. My brain
says "oh, the pointing thing" and my thumb gets ready. It's not a reason to stay, it's a reason to think "seen it".
What would keep me: something I don't know yet. The thing I didn't know is the blind-ish driver.
Better first line: **"Japan's most famous train habit started because one driver was losing his eyesight."**
(or keep the picture of the pointing driver and SAY the Hori fact over it.)

**2. "Signal... green!"** Fine. Short. It's the sound everyone recognises.

**3. "Tourists think he's arguing with traffic lights."** Small smile at most. It's the joke I already made in my head
on line 1, so it's late. Also the jump from this to "over a century ago" feels like a channel change.
Better, as a bridge: **"Tourists think he's lost his mind. But the story goes... it started with a driver losing his
eyesight."**

**4. "Over a century ago, the story goes, a steam train driver named Hori was losing his eyesight."** (8.4-13.2 s)
DANGER ZONE. "Over a century ago" + "the story goes" = history-class voice + "so it might be fake?" This is where I'd
swipe if I was on the fence. The eyesight part IS a "no way", but it's buried at the end of a 17-word sentence. Lead
with it: **"The story goes, a steam train driver named Hori was going... kinda blind."** (I know you can't say blind;
"was losing his eyesight" at the FRONT of the sentence works, "over a century ago" can be the board.)

**5. "So he shouted every signal to his fireman."** Small confusion: how does shouting fix bad eyes? I get it a second
later when the fireman shouts back (he's double-checking), but say it: **"So he yelled every signal to his fireman...
so the fireman could yell back if he got it wrong."** Or let the picture do it: fireman squints out the window too.

**6-7. "Signal... clear!" / "Clear!"** The fireman screaming it back with coal on his face is cute. Keep.

**8. "His boss rode along, called it genius... and made it the rule."** Good setup. I can feel the joke coming.

**9. "He never knew Hori was squinting."** REAL LAUGH. Best line in the script. A whole country's safety rule because
a boss thought a guy's bad eyes were a technique. Comment material: "the boss promoting the guy who can't see is the
most corporate thing ever". Give it the pause before it.

**10. "The finger came later."** DEAD. Four words of footnote. I don't care when the finger came, and it's confusing
(the video opened on the finger, so I assumed Hori did it). Cut it, or make it a laugh:
**"The finger got added later. Apparently yelling wasn't enough."**

**11. "But does shouting at a light do anything?"** Fine question, but my honest answer is "I don't really care yet".
The question that would grip me is personal: **"But does yelling at a light actually do anything? Somebody tested it."**

**12. "In 1994, Japan's railway lab set a trap."** "Set a trap" is good. "1994" is fine. OK.

**13. "Press the button on go. Never on red."** Who? Me? The volunteers? For a second I thought it was telling ME to
press something (like the clown quiz). Say who: **"Volunteers had one job: hit the button on go. Never on red."**
Actually... making it a mini quiz for the viewer could be fun, but only if you go all-in.

**14. "A hundred go lights. Then... one red."** Good. The pause and the red flood is a nice beat.

**15. "Of twelve who just looked, five pressed anyway."** OK "no way"-ish. Five of twelve is almost half, I buy it.
Twelve people is tiny though; someone in the comments WILL say "sample size 12".

**16. "Three of them did it again."** Real snort. Caught once, still did it. Keep it exactly like this.

**17. "Of twelve who pointed and called... not one."** This lands. 5 vs 0 is clean. This is the payoff of the whole
lab bit.

**18-20. "You see, by then, your hand's on autopilot. / Pointing and calling adds about a third of a second. / And
that pause beats autopilot."** (40.9-47.8 s) THIS IS WHERE I'D LEAVE. Seven seconds of explaining with nothing
happening, "autopilot" twice, and "a third of a second" sounds like a spec sheet. Sleepy guy + stopwatch is the most
boring picture in the film. Also, I already got it from line 17. One line max:
**"After a hundred greens, your hand stops asking your brain. Pointing makes it ask."**

**21. "Like staring at the stove... then going back to check."** I relate (that's literally me), but it's a little
backwards: it sounds like going back to check IS the pause. I had to think. Make it about me, and make it a dare:
**"It's why you can stare right at your stove... and two minutes later have no idea if it's off. Point at it next
time."** That last bit is the thing I'd actually try and then comment about.

**22. "He's not pointing at nothing. He's pointing his brain at it."** Slight cringe, fortune-cookie. And nobody in
the video ever said "nothing": line 1 says he points at a LIGHT, so this answers a question I never had. (It only works
if the title says "nothing", and titles don't get read out loud in my head.)
Better, paying off the tourists: **"So he's not yelling at the light. He's yelling at his own autopilot."**

**23. "Since 1996, New York's subway conductors must point too: at a striped board."** Okay, this woke me up. I'm
American, New York doing it is a real "wait, we do that??" moment. But "Since 1996" is a dead opener.
**"And New York copied it. Subway conductors there have to point at a zebra-striped board."**

**24. "So two pranksters held a sign under it."** The "So" doesn't make sense (they didn't do it BECAUSE of 1996).
**"So of course, New Yorkers did what New Yorkers do."** then the sign. Or: **"Then two New Yorkers held a sign under
it."**

**25. "Point here if New York is the greatest city in the world."** Smile. It's a good real sign.

**26. "And the conductors... had to point."** Smirk, not a laugh. It's a cute button but it's a NEW story with new
people in the last 12 seconds, and it has nothing to do with the experiment or Hori. Anti-theft's "stolen twice" twists
the thing I just learned. This one just adds a fun fact. Would land harder if it was quicker and drier:
**"And the conductors? Rules are rules."** (hold on the point).

## Quick answers

- **Is the first line a reason to stay?** No. It's a meme I've seen. The reason to stay is in line 4 (the guy losing
  his eyesight) and it arrives at 13 s, after I've probably gone.
- **Where I get bored:** 8-13 s ("over a century ago, the story goes...") and 41-48 s (the autopilot lecture). Second
  one is where I'd actually swipe.
- **Where I get confused:** line 5 (how does shouting help bad eyes?), line 10 (so Hori didn't point?), line 13 (who's
  pressing?), line 21 (the stove comparison is backwards for a second), line 22 ("nothing"? it was a light).
- **Unconvinced:** "the story goes" makes the best part sound made up. Twelve people per group feels small.
- **Real laughs:** 2. "He never knew Hori was squinting." "Three of them did it again." Smiles: the fireman yelling
  back, the sign.
- **"No way" moments:** a train driver losing his eyesight; 5 of 12 vs 0 of 12; New York does it too.
- **Does the ending give me something new?** Yes, technically (the prank is new), but it's a side story, not a twist on
  what I learned. I'd smile and swipe.
- **Finish it?** Maybe. If I survive to the lab I finish it because I want the number, but I'd leave around 41 s if
  the lecture plays as written.
- **Rewatch?** No. There's nothing to catch on a second look (the clown one has that, this doesn't).
- **Send it?** Maybe to one friend who's into Japan, or a New Yorker for the last bit. Not a group chat send.
- **What I'd comment:**
  - "the boss promoting the guy who couldn't see is peak corporate"
  - "I'm pointing at my stove tomorrow"
  - "sample size 12 lmao"
  - "conductors had to point is so New York"

## The one thing that would make it way better

Make it ONE story that starts with the part I don't know. Open on the squinting driver (not the pointing meme), keep
Hori as the guy we follow, and have the lab result feel like the payoff to HIS accident ("the boss was wrong about his
eyes... and right about the rule"). Then the New York prank is a quick bonus laugh, not a third story. Kill the
autopilot lecture; one line plus the stove dare is enough.
