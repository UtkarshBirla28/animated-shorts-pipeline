# Read 3 (story editor): POINTING v4 rev 3

## Ranking of all six (best first)
1. **Cart (Ricks Clips)**: a mystery object, a new event in every sentence, the payoff is in the last line. I never knew where it was going.
2. **Dash cam (Ricks Clips)**: one con man, the stakes rise each time, and a reversal you can see coming but still enjoy.
3. **Pink cell (Ricks Clips)**: a weird rule, proof, then the twist turns it around.
4. **Anti-theft**: the honest fiancee hook, one spine, and "stolen twice" pays off the first line.
5. **Clown**: the quiz grabs you at once and "did you see the money?" closes the loop. The middle is a pile of stats.
6. **Pointing v4 (new)**: the best version so far. The lab is real and gripping, and the dead-sexy sign is a real laugh. But it has three main characters, a detour and two endings, and the hook (his bad eyes) never gets paid off. It is close to the clown, but the clown's ending answers its opening and this one's doesn't.

## Line by line
- **L1** "eyes had gone bad. So he started shouting." This is good enough to stop for. The odd cause and effect makes me curious.
- **L3** "At least, that's the story." This kills the speed at second 3. Fold it into the name: *"Legend says his name was Hori. He shouted every signal to his coal man..."*
- **L4-5** These are fine. But nobody tells me *why* shouting helps bad eyes. The coal man is his spare pair of eyes, and the script makes me work that out myself. Say it: *"...so his coal man would check it for him."*
- **L6-7** "He never knew Hori's eyes were bad." This is the best line. It is the real twist: the boss thought it was brilliant discipline, but it was a man hiding his bad eyes. The script drops this and never comes back to it.
- **L10** "But does it actually work?" This line is dead and generic. Better: *"Sounds silly. So in 1994, Japan's railway lab tried to make people fail."* (it merges with L11).
- **L14** "Twelve people only looked at the light." When you only hear it, this is confusing. Better: *"Twelve people just watched. Five pressed anyway."*
- **L15-16** This is my "no way" moment: 5, then 3 again, then zero.
- **L17** This works. It is the one explanation, and it is short.
- **L18-19 (stove)** This is where I got bored. It is a new person in a new place, and the grammar is knotted ("stared at your stove, left... and had no idea if it's off"). Cut it. If you must keep it, use one line: *"Try it on your stove tonight."*
- **L20** "onto something... by accident" This is a fine ending, but then NY starts another story after it.
- **L21-24** The real laugh: "Point here if you are dead sexy" / "had to point." It works because the conductor *has to*.
- **L25** "Hori's boss would be so proud." This is weak. It pays off the boss, not the hook. Better: *"Millions of people with perfect eyes still point... because one man's eyes had gone bad."* That closes the loop on line 1 and leads into frame 0.

## Does the ending pay off the beginning?
No. The opening promises a secret (bad eyes). The ending is about the boss's pride and a prank. The spine also breaks in the middle: Hori shouted, but the lab tested pointing.

## Would I finish it?
Maybe. I would drift off at the stove (about 48 s). The NY sign would pull a few people back, but many would already be gone. Would I rewatch? No. Would I send it? Only the dead-sexy bit. My comment: *"wait, so nobody ever fixed his eyes??"* or *"pointing at my stove rn."*

## Biggest change
Use one spine: Hori hides his bad eyes, the boss makes the cover-up a rule, the world copies it, the lab proves it works, New York copies it too (the laugh), and the last line goes back to his eyes. Cut the stove, cut L20 and replace L25.

## Ready to record?
Not yet. It needs about 20 minutes of edits, not a rewrite: the hedge, L10, L14, the stove, and the ending. After that I would record it.
