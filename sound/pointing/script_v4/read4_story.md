# Read 4: story reader (blind)

## Ranking, the one I'd most want to watch first
1. **Pink Prison Cell.** It keeps escalating (the shade, the stopwatch, the arm wrestle, the forgotten guy), then the ending flips the whole premise.
2. **Anti-theft sign.** The honest fiancee is a hook you can't refuse. The test is clean, and "stolen twice" is a real last-second gift.
3. **Tree / target fixation.** Pure comic escalation. The sheriff hitting the stump is the best payoff of the six.
4. **Clown v6.** The play-along hook is strong, and "Did you see the money?" turns the whole film back on me. The middle has too many voices.
5. **Cart.** It's a mystery box: 82 holes, the rope, the human umbrella. It has no "why", but I can't leave.
6. **Pointing v4 (new).** Now it's coherent and has one big laugh. It's still three short stories stitched together, and nothing is at stake.

## Line by line
- **L1** "eyes had gone bad. So he started shouting." This is odd enough to make me pause, not stop. A blind driver at full speed is where the danger is, so say it that way.
- **L3** "The story goes, his name was Hori." The hedge is still at second 4. Use "Railway legend says his name was Hori." and drop the other hedge at the end.
- **L5-6** The boss loved it and never knew. I smirked. This is good.
- **L7** "And they point, too." Wait, who added pointing? The thing in the title shows up with no cause. Use "Somebody even added a point."
- **L9** The question is logical but a little abstract. I want the answer, but I don't need it. Try: "Their eyes are perfect. So why are they still doing it?"
- **L10-15** This is the strongest part. "Five pressed anyway" lands, and "Not one pressed" is my one "no way" moment.
- **L16** Yes, it answers L9, and "your hand stops waiting for your brain" is the best explaining line in the script.
- **L17** Cereal in the fridge got a smile. Keep it.
- **L18** "Pointing and saying it makes it wait." The "it" is muddy, and this is the moment to show the stakes. Try: "Pointing makes your hand wait for your eyes. On a train, that's the difference between stopping and running a red."
- **L19** "Even New York copied it." Flat. Try: "New York liked it so much, its conductors must point at this board before the doors open."
- **L20-22** "Dead sexy" got the only real laugh, and "had to point" lands it.
- **L23** "All because one driver's eyes went bad. Or so the story goes." It goes back to the start but adds nothing new. After the laugh it sags, and the hedge sags more. Try: "All because one driver's eyes went bad... and his boss never noticed." That pays off L6 and loops.

## Would I finish it?
Probably. I drifted at L7, where pointing comes from nowhere, and again at L18. The lab pulled me back. Would I rewatch it? No. Would I send it to someone? Maybe the New York part on its own. My comment would be "I'd point at that sign too."

## What separates it from my top three
In the top three, one chain of events keeps getting worse and the ending reverses or tops everything. Here, nobody is ever in danger. The origin is a legend, the proof is a button test, and the best moment (New York) is a side story. The fixes above (danger in L1 and L18, a cause for the point, an ending that pays off the boss) would get it to about 4th or 5th. They can't get it to the top three. That gap comes from the material: a safety habit that works has no disaster and no twist. Another rewrite has hit diminishing returns.

**Ready to record:** yes, after the small fixes to L3, L7, L18 and L23. Don't do a fifth restructure.
