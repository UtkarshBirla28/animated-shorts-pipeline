# Read 2: plain viewer (26, India, English second language, sound low, captions on)

## Ranking, most wanted to watch first
1. **Cart and the army.** A mystery from the first second ("82 holes... why?"), and every line makes it stranger. Simple words. "Are we fighting them or grating cheese?" made me laugh.
2. **Pink prison cell.** The arm-wrestle is a real "wait, what?" moment, and the twist at the end flips everything.
3. **Railroad gaps.** Easy to follow even with no sound: the gaps shrink, then the snake rail. "Even your gaps know you were wrong" is funny.
4. **Anti-theft sign.** The paperclip hook is great and "stolen twice" is a perfect ending. But "heritage", "vandalized" and "petrified" are hard for me.
5. **Pointing v4 (new).** It finally feels like one story, and the lab part is strong. But it starts with something I have already seen in reels, and then it jumps to New York at the end.
6. **Clown.** The quiz is fun, but after it there are too many numbers and groups. "A call pulls your mind toward someone who isn't there" lost me.

**New script: 5 of 6.** It is close to Anti-theft, but its last laugh is weaker.

## Line by line
- **1-2.** "Japan's train drivers point at signals... and shout." I know this already. My thumb is ready to scroll.
- **3.** "The story goes, it started with..." is long, and it has two idioms ("the story goes", "had gone bad"). It's understandable, but I read it twice.
- **4-7.** The shout and the shout back are good, and the captions carry it fine. "Shovelling his coal" is OK.
- **8-9.** "He never knew Hori's eyes were bad." **Real smile.** This is the best joke in the script.
- **10.** "The pointing came later." **Dead line.** It's a footnote and it stops the story.
- **11-12.** "Set a trap" makes me want to see it. Clear.
- **13.** "A hundred times, it wasn't." Wasn't what? With sound low I had to go back to "red". With no sound, the music stopping is lost, but the red room carries it.
- **14.** "Twelve people just watched the light." For me "just" can mean "a moment ago". "Five pressed anyway" was a **"no way"** moment.
- **15.** That's a lot of numbers coming fast. I can drop this line and lose nothing.
- **16.** "Not one pressed." **No way, second time.** It's the strongest moment.
- **17.** "Hori's boss was right... by accident." A nice callback, and it ties the story together.
- **18.** "Your hand stops waiting for your brain." This is the best line. It's simple and I believe it. This is not a lecture.
- **19-21.** The stove is very relatable (gas stove, every Indian mum). I would try it.
- **22.** Here I get bored and confused. New York comes from nowhere, it feels like a new story, and "conductor" for me means the bus ticket man.
- **23-25.** The sign gag is cute but I only smiled. "Had to point" works with captions.

**Would I finish it?** Maybe. The two places I would leave are line 1 (I've seen it before) and line 22 (new topic). I would not rewatch it. I might send it to my mother for the stove tip.

**My comment:** "Hori's boss invented safety by accident 😂" or "Pointing at my stove from today."

## Dead lines and better ones
- "The pointing came later. But does any of it actually work?" → "Later they added the point. But does shouting at a light really work?"
- "A hundred times, it wasn't. Then... one red." → "A hundred lights. No red. Then... red."
- "Twelve people just watched the light." → "Twelve people only looked."
- "New York copied it: subway conductors must point at a striped board before opening the doors." → "Even New York copied it. Before the doors open, the train guard must point at this board."
- "So two New Yorkers held a sign under it:" → "So two pranksters held a sign right under it:"
- "The story goes, it started with a steam-train driver named Hori... whose eyes had gone bad." → "The story goes: a steam-train driver named Hori was losing his eyesight."

## Opening: B, with a better first line
A opens with the fact everyone knows, so I scroll. B opens on the part I didn't know, and a driver with bad eyes is a mystery: how is he driving? But "So, the story goes, he started shouting" is clunky to read. My first line would be: **"This train driver couldn't see the signals anymore... so he started shouting at them."** Line 2 of B is also too long. Cut it to "His name was Hori. He shouted every signal to his coal man."

## Last sign: 2
"Point here if you are dead sexy" got an actual laugh: a serious man in uniform *has* to point at you. "Greatest city in the world" means something only to New Yorkers. "Dead sexy" is a common meme phrase in India. The sign is on screen, so I get it even with no sound.
