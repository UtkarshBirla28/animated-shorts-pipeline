# Read 2: story editor (blind, as a viewer)

## Ranking, most wanted to least
1. **One Cart Stopped an Army**: a secret object, a slap, a man lying on the cart in the rain. Every line is a new event, and the payoff explains every odd thing he did.
2. **Perfect Railroads**: a boss who mocks him, a public loss, the heat building, and the other line kicks sideways. The stakes keep going up.
3. **Anti-Theft Sign**: the honest fiancee whispering "we'd better get ours now" is the hook. "Stolen twice" is a great last line.
4. **Pink Prison Cell**: weird from the first second and the arm wrestle is a real laugh. It ends on an explanation, not on an event.
5. **Pointing v4 (new)**: now one real spine (bad eyes, the boss copies it, and 80 years later he turns out right by accident). The lab section is the best stretch in the file. It still has two endings after its payoff.
6. **Clown v6**: the quiz hook and the money reveal are clever, but the middle is a list of numbers and survey answers.

**New script: 5 of 6.** It beats the clown script on story. It loses to the anti-theft script because that one ends on its strongest line and this one doesn't.

## Line by line
- **1-2 (A):** I've seen this one. Japanese pointing is a known meme, so this is where I'd scroll.
- **3** "The story goes, it started with a steam-train driver named Hori..." The hedge comes first and the hook last. Better: *"It started with a steam-train driver whose eyes were going bad."* Put "the story goes" in line 4 if it has to stay.
- **4-7:** Good. A fun call-and-response, easy to picture.
- **8-9** "He never knew Hori's eyes were bad." This is the first real laugh. Hold it.
- **10** "The pointing came later." This line is dead. It's a footnote spoken out loud. "Does any of it actually work?" is a soft question. Better: *"So was the boss a genius... or just lucky?"* That sets up line 17 exactly.
- **11-13:** Tight, and a real trap. "Then... one red." works.
- **14** "Five pressed anyway." This is the no-way moment.
- **15** "Three pressed again." I smirked. This is the funniest fact in the lab part.
- **16** "Not one pressed." It lands.
- **17** "Hori's boss was right... by accident." This is the real payoff of the opening. It's the line people will repeat.
- **18:** Short and clear. It doesn't feel like a lecture anymore. Keep it.
- **19-21 (stove):** I got bored here. The story is over and now I'm getting a tip. It also starts on a shaky "It's why" (the lab measured hand habit, and the stove is about memory). Cut it, or shrink it to one line after the subway gag.
- **22** "New York copied it: subway conductors must point at a striped board before opening the doors." Too long and too procedural. Better: *"New York copied it. Conductors have to point at this board before the doors open."*
- **23-25:** A real laugh. "And the conductors... had to point." is a good button.

**Would I finish it?** With opening A, maybe. I'd leave at line 2 or drift at line 10. With B, yes, but I'd feel the stove dip. I'd rewatch the lab part, not the whole video. I'd send it for the subway sign. My comment would be: *"So the entire system exists because one guy needed glasses and his boss thought it was a vibe."* That comment is better than line 17. Consider ending the Hori half on *"All of it... because one man's eyes went bad."*

## Opening: B
"A train driver's eyes went bad. So he started shouting." That's a question I haven't heard answered before: why would bad eyes make a man shout? A starts on the one fact everyone already knows. Tighten B's first line by dropping "the story goes" from it. Line 2 of B (21 words) needs splitting. Note for picture: with B the loop "a point into a point" breaks, because frame 0 becomes the steam engine. End on Hori peering, or open on a point.

## Last sign: 2
"Point here if you are dead sexy" is shorter and needs no thought. The conductor is forced to call himself sexy, and the grin pays it off. The New York pride sign is a smile. The sexy sign is a laugh. It's still family-safe enough for a cartoon.

## Biggest change
Make line 17 the last story beat. Then go straight to the subway sign as the final laugh, and cut the stove tip. That leaves one story and one encore.
