# Read 4: comedy reader, POINTING v4 rev 4

## Ranking (the one I'd most want to watch first)
1. **Pink Prison Cell.** One weird act, rising strangeness, then a reversal: "it does the exact opposite."
2. **One Cart / Army.** 82 holes is a puzzle you can't skip, the "grating cheese" line gets a laugh, and the payoff is huge.
3. **Anti-theft sign.** A killer first line, a clear mystery, and "stolen twice" as a second twist.
4. **Perfect Railroads.** One stubborn engineer and a boss who's sure he's right. The ending is a little guessable but it lands.
5. **Clown v6.** The quiz grabs me, the middle is heavy on numbers, and "Did you see the money?" is a real sting.
6. **Pointing v4 (new).** Better built than before, with one big laugh. But it's a tour of three stories, and the ending only repeats line 1.

## Line by line
- **L1** "This train driver's eyes had gone bad. So he started shouting." It works and the "So" is funny. Would I stop scrolling for it? Maybe, not definitely.
- **L3** "The story goes, his name was Hori." This is clunky and the hedge still trips me. Try: **"Legend says his name was Hori."**
- **L5** "One day his boss rode along, loved it, and made it a rule." Flat. Try: **"Then his boss rode along, thought it was genius, and made everyone do it."**
- **L6** "He never knew Hori's eyes were bad." This is the best comic seed in the script: a whole rule built on a cover-up. Nothing ever comes back to it.
- **L7** "And they point, too." Dead tag. Try: **"Now they point at them too."**
- **L9** "But why would good eyes need a trick for bad ones?" I had to parse it, so it reads clever more than it makes me curious. Try: **"But today's drivers can see fine. So why are they still doing it?"**
- **L10-15** are the strongest stretch. "Set a trap" pulls me in, the 98-99-100-red beat works, and the clipboard drop is a small smile. "Not one pressed" was my "no way."
- **L16** answers L9 cleanly: "It was never about the eyes." Good.
- **L17** the cereal in the fridge gets a smile of recognition. Keep it.
- **L18** "Pointing and saying it makes it wait." I can't tell who "it" is. Try: **"Point and say it, and your hand has to wait for you."**
- **L19-22** "Point here if you are dead sexy" / "And the conductors... had to point." This was a real laugh, the only one. It's a detour from the eyes, but I don't care.
- **L23** "All because one driver's eyes went bad. Or so the story goes." Dead. It recaps line 1 and doesn't pay it off, so I leave on a shrug, and the hedge is the last thing I hear. Pay off L6 instead: **"So a whole country still copies a man who was just hiding his bad eyes."** That still loops into frame 0.

## Would I finish, rewatch, share?
I'd finish it, because the lab trap keeps me there, but I'd drift around L5-7. I wouldn't rewatch it. I'd send it to a friend for the New York sign, not for the story. My comment would be: "the conductor grinning lmao". Nobody would comment about Hori.

## What still separates it from the top three
The top three follow one person and one mystery all the way through, and their endings change what you thought you knew: the pink backfires, the rockets can't miss, the wood gets stolen twice. This one moves Hori to the lab to New York. The lab's answer, that pointing makes you pay attention, is impressive but half-guessable. It confirms what you suspected instead of turning it over.

The fixes above (L3, L9, L18, and an ending that cashes in "the boss never knew") would lift it to about 4th or 5th. That's a real gain. The rest of the gap is the material itself. The true story has no reversal, and no edit to this story can invent one honestly.

## Ready to record?
No, not as written: the ending is dead. After the four small fixes, yes. None of them needs new facts.
