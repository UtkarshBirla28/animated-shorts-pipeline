## A vs B
**First 3 seconds:** B wins. "Tap your foot every time this light turns green" gives me a job. A's "eyes had gone bad, so he started shouting" is OK but slow.

**Would I play along with B?** Yes, with my foot, on the bus. Tapping my foot is smart, because tapping the screen would pause the video. Things that could go wrong:
- If the voice says "Go" when the light turns red, I feel cheated, not tricked. I would comment "the voice said go".
- With the sound low I read the captions, not the lamp. "Go. Go. Go." on screen pulls my eyes off the light.
- Five greens is not enough. I will not be on autopilot yet, so I won't tap, and the trick fails.

**Where I'd swipe:** In A, at A3 ("Legend says...") and again at A9, before the lab. In B, maybe at B11-B13, when it goes back to history after the test is done. "He never knew about the eyes" pulls me back.

**Laughs and "no way" moments:** Both have the cereal in the fridge, the dropped clipboard and the New York sign. B has one more "no way" moment, and it's about me: "I tapped!" "The boss never knew" hits harder in A because it comes early.

**Ending:** B. "Now try the lights again. This time... point." makes me replay right away. I'd type: "I tapped on red, bro" or "didn't tap, I'm built different". A's ending is a neat circle, but I would not comment on it.

## Winner: B
B makes me part of the experiment. A only tells me about one. Its opening gives me a task in second one, and its loop gives me a reason to watch again.

## Must-fix in B
- **B2 "Go. Go. Go. Go. Go..."**: Add more greens (8 or more, quick) so the habit builds. Leave the red beat silent, with no caption. Replacement: "Go. Go. Go. Go. Go. Go. Go. Go." Then [red, a beep, no word].
- **B9 "After a hundred times, your hand stops waiting for your brain. It's how the cereal ends up in the fridge."**: Too long for one caption. Split it: "After a hundred times, your hand stops waiting for your brain." / "That's how cereal ends up in the fridge."
- **B13 "And the story goes, the shouting began with a steam-train driver whose eyes had gone bad."**: Clumsy, and "gone bad" is unclear. Replacement: "The story goes, it all started with a train driver who couldn't see well."
- **B16 "He never knew about the eyes."**: Too soft. Replacement: "He never knew the driver could barely see."
- **B18 "Point here if you are dead sexy."**: To me "dead" sounds like death. Replacement: "So two New Yorkers held a cheeky sign right under it." Keep the real words big on the sign so people can read them.
