# Round 5 read: story editor

## Ranking, all seven (most want to watch first)
1. **Cart stopped an army**: pure mystery engine. 82 holes, the rope nobody may touch, a man lying on the cart in the rain. Every line is a new odd act, and the reveal pays off every one of them.
2. **Tree you crash into**: a funny escalation (arrow, tow-truck guy in a lawn chair, the sheriff, the stump), and the last line is a kicker.
3. **Pink prison cell**: an absurd setup, a "wait, what?", then a reversal at the end.
4. **Anti-theft sign (ours)**: the best hook we have, a real voice ("We'd better get ours now"), and "stolen twice" is a true second twist.
5. **Clown (ours)**: the money reveal is played on you without you knowing, so it can't fail. The middle is a list of interview answers.
6. **B**: a strong lab test, two real laughs, and a loop that asks you to replay. But the opening game is easy to dodge.
7. **A**: a soft legend for 25 s before any question. The lab test is the only part with grip, and it arrives too late.

## A vs B
**First 3 seconds.** B. "Tap your foot every time this light turns green" is an order with a light on screen, so I look. "This train driver's eyes had gone bad" is a mild curiosity I can swipe past.

**Would I play along with B?** Half of me would. My foot won't move, but my eyes will follow the lamp. The danger: five greens build no habit, and "Don't tap on red" was said two seconds earlier, so most people win. Then "Did you tap?" falls flat, and the viewer feels smart instead of caught. A fix: the voice keeps saying "Go" on the red. The trap becomes the narrator's rhythm, not the lamp, and that one catches people.

**Where I'd swipe.** A: at A5-A7 (the boss, the rule, a century later). It's history with no question yet. B: at B11-B13. The test is over, the moral has landed, and then a new era starts. It feels like the video ended and a second one began.

**Laughs and "no way" moments.** About even on laughs: cereal in the fridge and "dead sexy" are in both. B wins on "no way" because the bad eyes come as a surprise origin after you already know the science. In A you know the eyes from line 1, so the boss line is just a fact.

**Ending.** A's circle ("a boss who never noticed") is neat but quiet. B's "This time... point" sends me back to frame 0 to try it, and that replay is real. I'd type: "I pointed at my phone screen like an idiot" or "my foot moved on the red, I'm done."

## Winner: B
The test comes first and it's aimed at me, so the best part of this story is also the hook. A spends its first third on the weakest material.

## Must-fix in B
- **B1-B2** "Tap your foot every time this light turns green. Don't tap on red." / "Go. Go. Go. Go. Go..."
  Too few beats, and the voice goes quiet on red, which warns you. Replace with: "Tap on green. Not red. Ready? Go. Go. Go. Go. Go. Go. Go." Seven quick greens, and the last "Go" lands on the red.
- **B3** "Did you tap?"
  A full tap is a high bar, so people who didn't tap feel smug. Replace with: "Be honest. Did your foot move?"
- **B11-B13** "Japan's train drivers have done it for a century... And the story goes, the shouting began with a steam-train driver whose eyes had gone bad."
  This needs to open a new question, not continue a recap. Replace with: "Japan's train drivers do it at every signal. But it didn't start as science. The story goes, it started with a driver hiding bad eyes."
- **B16** "He never knew about the eyes."
  Vague. Replace with A's line: "He never knew the driver couldn't see."
- **B20** "Now try the lights again. This time... point."
  Fine, but tighten it so the loop snaps: "Now go back. This time... point."

## Steal from A
- The cold-open shout "Signal clear!" over a speeding cab. Use it as B13's first sound so the steam era hits hard.
- "...and a boss who never noticed" as B16's last beat.
- The kid at the front window copying the point. Cheap, warm, and it gives the viewer a stand-in.
