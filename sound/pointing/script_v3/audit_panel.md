# Audit: the viewer panel and the spoken lines (pointing, SCRIPT_v3_draft)

Independent auditor, 2026-11-19. I did not write the draft. Nothing was rendered, played or recorded.
Lens: 8 real people meeting the film cold in the Shorts feed, on BOTH hooks; then every spoken line judged as
speech (does it sound written, is it clear to a non-native speaker, can an AI voice say it).

**This is a forecast, not data.** Eight imagined viewers cannot measure a swipe rate. Every "leaves at" time below is
tied to a named cause in the draft, so the lead can check the reasoning. The panel is kinder than the feed (two of the
eight almost never leave), so read the shares as a way to compare, not as retention numbers.

Read for this audit: the draft in full (all 671 lines); the three judge files in full; `PLAYBOOK.md` sections 3-8;
`YOUTUBE_STUDY.md` section 14; `HOOK_RESEARCH.md` sections 0-11; `OPENINGS_DATA.md` sections 4-8; candidates B and C
(frame 0, first 5 s, full script); `voice/NOTES.md`; the measured v1 track (`voice/_full/words.json`,
`phrases.json`); `BIBLE_NOTES.md` sections 9-12; `LESSONS.md` 29 and 36.

## 4. Where people leave, and why

**A. 45.75-58.55 s: the tourist is gone for 12.8 s, the laughs stop for 20 s, and the ending is already known.**
This is the only place the lead-hook panel loses anyone. Lead decision 3 exists for this exact risk. The draft meets
it on paper (the stretched call, "Bag... check!" and "Bag... gone." are all after 57 s), but between 45.75 and 57.8 s
the viewer gets: jars (7.75 s), a 1.10 s insert of the bag and the boy, then 3.95 s of the driver alone under a
moral. The hook judge's fix for C was "a 1.0-1.2 s shot of the doorway between the jars and the cab line, or cut
the jars to about 6 s". The draft took the insert, kept the jars at 7.75 s, and then spent 3.95 s more in the cab
(the first word of the stretched call is now said there), so the tourist's absence grew from C's 10.7 s to 12.8 s.

**B. The entry to the lab is a method sentence, and the best join in the film is buried.** Both judges named the
same graft: the dare "What's a finger gonna do?" cut straight to "Japan's railway lab tested it." (viewer judge:
"the best join in any middle"). The draft lists it as taken, but plays the dare from a phone speaker 18 dB under the
narrator, during his sentence "His mind was on his phone.". At that level it is either not heard, and the join is
lost, or it is heard, and it masks the one sentence that explains the film. Then the narrator says "Japan's railway
lab ran the same test two ways.": nine words of method. P7 and P1 hear school. P2 asks "the same as what?".

**C. On mute the lab has no tie to the platform.** The melody is the clock and the tie, and it is sound only. The
draft's own note in section 5.5 says the lab "leans on its captions".

**D. Smaller, no one leaves:**
- 9.15-14.85 s: two narrator lines in a row right after the hook. The foot hook (11.5 s) and the cap (about 14 s)
  carry it. Keep both gags readable; they are why nobody left here.
- 3.05-4.45 s: the jump back in time is carried by a face match, the past tense, and a suitcase revealed by a crane
  move, all in 1.40 s. Seven of eight follow it at once.
- The boy points in silence at 35.45 s and only speaks at 61.1 s. Viewers will type "why didn't the kid just yell?".
  That is a good comment IF the film shows that it is the point: the silent finger failed, the finger plus the shout
  turns his head, one train too late. The draft has all the pieces; two small staging words make it read (section 7,
  improvement 4).

## 7. IMPROVEMENTS (polish, each with the exact fix)

1. **A picture of the melody for muted viewers.** From the first note of the departure melody to its last, show one
   small pulsing music-note pictogram in a top corner (caption layer), on the platform and over the jars; the six
   balls drop on its pulses. A pictogram is allowed by the culture guard, nothing blinks on the train, and the lab is
   then tied to the platform with the sound off.
2. **"Bag... gone." must be heard.** In the mix hold t07 no more than 6 dB under the narrator's average level. Keep
   it "small" with flat pitch, not with level. Keep the small lower-case caption at 70 % of the house size or more.
3. **Never record a two-word narrator line alone.** Record n09 and n10 as ONE take, "Second stop. ... It stays.",
   and split it in silence, as the draft already does for n06-n08.
4. **Make the boy's silence and his shout read as cause and effect.** In p01 (35.45 s) write "mouth shut". In k01
   write that the tourist's head starts to turn ON the word "check!" (about 62.0 s), not after it. Then "why didn't
   the kid yell?" has its answer on screen: when he does, it works, one train too late.
5. **The jump back in time (3.05-4.45 s).** Hold the matched laughing face for 10 frames before the camera starts
   to sink, and finish the sink by 4.15 s, so the suitcase at his knee stands still for the last 9 frames of the
   shot.
6. **Give the deadpan a breath if the take allows.** Set the gap before "He is not." to 0.20-0.25 s on any take
