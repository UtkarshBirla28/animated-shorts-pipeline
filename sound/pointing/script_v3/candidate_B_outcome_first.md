## 1. Title, angle, pitch

- **Title:** Why Japanese Train Drivers Point at Nothing (kept: 43 characters, a plain curiosity gap).
- **Angle (one sentence):** Frame 0 shows the loss itself (his yellow suitcase rides away on a train while he stands
  on the platform), the narrator states it flat and names the tiny cause ("All he did was laugh at a finger"), and the
  film then runs the one minute that explains it and ends one frame before frame 0.
- **Why a viewer cannot swipe (one line):** In 2.5 s you have watched a man lose his suitcase and in 4.8 s you have
  been told all he did was laugh at a finger, so you stay to learn how a finger costs a suitcase, and from 17 s the
  suitcase sits in an open train doorway where you can see it and he cannot.

- **Colour:** red cap against a cream platform column, yellow against the stainless car side, a bright frame (no dark

**Three honest weaknesses of this script**

1. **It spends the ending's surprise at frame 0.** The viewer knows the bag will go. After the six-to-one answer at
   43 s only the "how" is open, and a sharp viewer has guessed that since the bag plant at 17 s. The last 20 s must

   shocks: it has to earn its laugh as him doing the driver's gesture. If that picture work is weak, expect a dip at
   43-57 s. The research says outcomes told first do not spoil enjoyment, but that study measured liking, not
   staying, and our own evidence is one Short.
2. **The hook is the fastest 4 seconds this channel has planned, and it is tight on words.** Three cuts by 3.95 s, a
   jump back in time at 2.6 s and a look into the lens go against the owner's calm-pacing note and could confuse on a
   small phone; the look into the lens is a test idea, not a finding. The title's promise (a driver pointing) is not
   on screen until 3.95 s. Ten words by 3 s is one above the floor of the 24 measured hits and below their median,
   and it drops to 9 if James reads the two short sentences slowly (his short lines measured 2.4-3.4 syllables/s);
