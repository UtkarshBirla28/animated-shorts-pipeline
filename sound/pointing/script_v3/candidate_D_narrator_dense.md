# Why Japanese Train Drivers Point at Nothing: script v3, CANDIDATE D ("narrator dense")

Written 2026-11-19 by the script writer for angle D. Nothing was rendered, played or recorded for this file.
The Deven Seenath study was about 85 % complete when this was written (a later pass re-checks the winner).

## 1. Title, angle, pitch

- **Title:** Why Japanese Train Drivers Point at Nothing (kept: 43 characters, a plain curiosity gap). Backup: "Why
  Japan's Train Drivers Talk to Themselves".
- **Angle D in one sentence:** copy the measured opening of the top channel as closely as honesty allows: the NARRATOR
  speaks from 0.05 s, the first sentence states the strange act as a plain fact, a cut to the hook object lands at
  1.0 s, a second force is on screen by 2.6 s, and after that the story drips one new thing every 3-4 s, with the
  character lines used as short punctuation between narrator lines.
- **Why a viewer cannot swipe (one line):** in the first 3 seconds a man in white gloves points straight at you and
  shouts at a green light with a thousand people behind him, and by 11 seconds you have been promised that the tourist
  laughing at him will end up pointing at his own suitcase, so you stay to watch it leave.

**Voice direction (so the AI voices sound real and stay apart)**

- **James (narrator), high-low-high on every line.** First sentence: start high and bright, peak on "SHOUTS at it",
  then drop low and slow on "with a thousand people", and lift a little on "behind him". The tease: low and dry on
  "Give him a minute.", up on "pointing too...", then down with a smile on "at his own suitcase." "Actually...": a
  small pause, then flat and pleased. The stove line: soft and close. The answer: slow down on "six" and "about one".
  "Which is why...": left hanging, pitch held up.
- **Masaki (driver):** calm, crisp, firm; name, a held beat of 0.35-0.45 s, then a clipped status with a falling end.
  "Green!" is the status word alone. His tagged reads rushed "Speed check" in the tests, so record "Speed... check!"
  untagged first.
- **Flackoo (tourist), six different energies:** a burst of real laughter; small and warm to his bag; loud bragging;
  a stiff, lower mock of the driver; a laughing dare; then tiny and flat on "Bag... gone." (the contrast is the joke).

- **Kavya (the boy):** "Check!" bright and a beat late; "Bag... check!" in the driver's exact rhythm with a proud rise.

**Three honest weaknesses of this script**

1. **The hook leads with the famous picture, and the new parts come later than in v2.** Frame 0 and the first sentence

   risk). The parts nobody has seen arrive later: the tourist speaks at 5.3 s (v2: 2.3 s) and the suitcase tease lands
   at 7-11 s, not by 3 s as YOUTUBE_STUDY 14B-0 R1 asks. The second force on screen by 3 s is a laugh and a number
   ("a thousand people"), not a visible threat like the pirate ship or the oncoming train of the top openings.
2. **The measured numbers are met on paper, at the edge of what James can do, and they bend the owner's calm rule.**
   12 words by 3 s needs James at about 4.1 words per second on a 12-word clause; on the brief's flat 3.1 the counts

   1.0 s and 2.6 s), against the owner's "hold 2.5 s, not too fast" direction, and it needs a new frame-0 drawing
   and camera. The owner has not approved that exception.
3. **A narrator-heavy film gives up some of what the owner likes most.** The narrator has 117 of the 153 words (76 %),
