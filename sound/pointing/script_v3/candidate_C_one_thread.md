# Why Japanese Train Drivers Point at Nothing: CANDIDATE C, "one thread, more story" (script v3 candidate, 2026-11-19)

## 1. Title, angle, pitch

- **Title:** Why Japanese Train Drivers Point at Nothing (unchanged; the first spoken line repeats it: "He points at nothing.")
- **Angle C in one sentence:** one chain of cause and effect, played as a silent comedy in one place (a Tokyo train and
  its platform): a laughing tourist, a suitcase that keeps trying to leave, and a small boy whose pointing finger saves
  the suitcase once and is ignored the second time; the science lands as the turn of that story, not as a lecture.
- **Why a viewer cannot swipe:** by 4.7 s they have been told that the laughing tourist is about to lose his suitcase,
  and for the next minute that yellow suitcase sits in an open train doorway while he looks everywhere else.

What changed from SCRIPT_v2, in short: the narrator now speaks from frame 2 (v2 had 4 words by 3 s); the outcome is
stated first; New York and the kitchen are gone; the lab is one 7.6 s picture of the two jars; a new first-stop gag
(the boy's finger saves the suitcase) makes the loss at the end a result of the tourist ignoring a finger, not an
accident; the last picture (the tourist pointing at an empty track) cuts into frame 0 on the line "He points at
nothing."

## 2. FRAME 0

**Place:** the driver's cab, golden light. The camera is inside the cab at the windscreen, at chest height, looking
BACK at the driver. Medium close-up, three-quarter front view (the driver card's "3/4 front-right" view).

**The one focal point:** the driver's WHITE glove, index finger out, coming straight at the lens. In frame 0 the arm is
already about 80 % out; it lands at full stretch on frame 3-4 with a small overshoot and is rock steady by frame 8.
The glove ends up about a quarter of the frame height, a little soft because it is so close.

**The face:** right behind the glove, sharp: navy cap, eyes locked just past the lens (they led the finger by 3
frames), jaw set, mouth closed (face "focus"). The face is about 22 % of the frame height. Glove and face are in full
low sun from ahead, so they are the brightest things in the frame.

**Behind him (one layer, a little soft and a little darker):** the cream partition and its window. In the window: the
tourist's face, red cap, mouth wide open in a laugh that is already bouncing in frame 0, cobalt phone held up. Lower in
the same window: the boy's too-big toy driver's cap and his small hand, which copies the point 6 frames late. At the
bottom edge of the window: a thin sliver of sunflower yellow (the top of the suitcase). That sliver is the reward for
people who watch twice.

**Colour:** navy and bright white on warm gold; one red cap; one yellow sliver. No phone screen frame, no title, no
logo, no text except the caption "He points" in the house caption band.

**Motion already under way in frame 0:** the glove's thrust; the driver's shoulder following it; the tourist's laugh
bounce; a mast shadow starting to sweep across the cab; a camera push-in of about 0.5 % per frame (the face grows about
10 % in the first second).

**First sound:** on frames 0-2 the glove's cotton "fwip", exactly on the snap. Under it, from frame 0, the tourist's
real laugh (a laugh-only take, about 10 dB under the voice) and the cab's motor whine. The narrator's first word
("He") starts at 0.07 s (frame 2). No music yet.

**Three honest weaknesses of this script**

1. **The opening only just clears the density floor, and it gives up v2's shout.** 10 and 16 words sit between the
   floor (9 and 14) and the median (11.5 and 19) of the big openings, and the 10 depends on James reading the first
   sentence in about 1.1 s; a slow take gives 9. The driver's shout, the best thing about v2's cold open, now arrives
   at 4.85 s; frame 0 has a glove snap, a laugh and the narrator instead, which goes against `YOUTUBE_STUDY.md` 14B
   item 1 ("keep the cold open"). Line 1 also says "nothing" over a front view that cannot show the empty track until
   4.8 s, and the line is only how it looks, not what is true. Shot 1 is 1.3 s, shorter than the owner's 2.5 s hold.
   This should be tested against a shout-first cut (D: "Signal, green!" 0.05-0.9 s, then the same narrator lines:
   about 9 and 14 words).
2. **It leans on animation, not on words.** 136 words is under the brief's 150-175, and about 19 s is picture and
   sound only. The suitcase rolling, the boy's point, the half-second where the tourist's eyes land on the suitcase,
   and the finger guns all have to read at phone size. If the look-right-at-it beat is not clear, "his eyes looked
   right at it" has no proof and the turn fails. The gags also need new poses and animation (section 5), and the
   pointing gag is used three times and "Doors... closed!" twice: played with the same energy, they will feel like
   repeats, which the study names as a cause of drops.
3. **The number is still a cutaway, and the film now has less to argue about.** The jars take 7.6 s away from the
   three characters at 70-81 %, with the narrator speaking in fragments; "six mistakes ... about one" could be heard
   as counts from one day, not a ratio. Without New York and the stove there are fewer surprising facts and one fewer
   proven comment trigger in the film itself (both now live only in the pinned comment). And because the outcome is
   told at 4 s, the ending surprises only by how it happens: "Bag... check!" and the small "Bag... gone." carry the
   whole payoff.
