# Judge review: the viewer panel (pointing, script v3 candidates A-D)

Written 2026-11-19 by an independent judge. I wrote none of the four candidates. Nothing was rendered, played or
recorded. Lens: 8 simulated people meeting each film cold in the Shorts feed.

**This is a forecast, not data.** Eight imagined viewers cannot measure a swipe rate. What the panel can do is show
WHERE each script gives a real person a reason to leave, and which lines fail by ear. Every "swipes at" time below
is tied to a named cause in the script, so the lead can check my reasoning.

Read for this review: all four candidates in full; `YOUTUBE_STUDY.md` sections 3, 4, 14; `HOOK_RESEARCH.md`;
`OPENINGS_DATA.md`; `SCRIPT_v2.md`; `BIBLE_NOTES.md` 1-5 and 7-10; `notes_students.md` parts B-E; `LESSONS.md`;
`voice/NOTES.md`; and the measured v1 voice track `voice/_full/phrases.json` and `words.json` (to test each
candidate's timing claims).

### Lines as spoken
- "He points at nothing." Clear to everyone, five syllables. Mismatch with the picture (see above).
- "And the tourist laughing at him is about to lose his suitcase." Plain, one breath, no idiom. Good.
- **"One is laughing. One is learning."** The most AI-sounding line in all four scripts: a balanced pair with
  alliteration. The owner's dialogue note names exactly this kind of line. Cut it or say it plainly.
- **"...and one small finger saves it." / "The small finger points again..."** In Indian and British English "small
  finger" means the little finger. Say "the boy's finger" or "the boy".
- "Over a thousand people ride behind that finger." A little written, but short and you can picture it. Keep or
  swap for B's plainer line.
- "There's nothing there!" Two "th" clusters around a laugh. Test it for clarity; "He's pointing at nothing!" was
  easier to say, but the narrator already used that phrase.
- "Bad suitcase." The best new character line in any candidate. Two words a real person says.
- "It stays." The best narrator joke in any middle.
- "This kid gets it!" An idiom; works because the finger guns carry it.
- "Just looking: six mistakes. (beat) Point and say it out loud: about one." The clearest form of the number for
  the ear and for captions. It can be heard as a count from one day, not a ratio: add "Same test." before it.
- Five of the eleven narrator lines describe what we can see ("First stop.", "The suitcase rolls for the door...",
  "He hops out for a better shot.", "It stays.", "The small finger points again..."). Dry and clear in small doses;
  over 40 s it can sound like a picture book to a teenager. One of them can be picture only.

### Strengths
1. The only middle with no lecture stretch and no cut away from the three characters for 45 s.
2. The first-stop near miss (13.2-19.2 s) turns a known outcome into "is it now?" and pays a laugh inside 6 s.
3. "What's a finger gonna do?" lands 5 s after a finger saved his bag: the film's question becomes a joke on him.
4. "Stay." / "It stays." and the bag alone for a full 2 s: the clearest bag plant of the four.
5. The stretched call "Doors... (the doors shut, 1.5 s) ...closed!" is 1.5 s of dread the viewer fills in.
6. Reads on mute; calm cuts (22 framings, average 2.98 s).

### Weaknesses
1. Frame 0 and line 1 are the famous fact (HOOK_RESEARCH rule 5). It lost P7 at 1.6 s.
2. It leans on animation: about 19 s of picture only. If the roll to the door, the look down the boy's arm and the
   finger guns do not read on flat cards at phone size, the turn ("his eyes looked right at it") has no proof.
3. The lab is tied to the story by sound only (lost P6 at 47 s).
4. It drops three things the study's keep list names (YOUTUBE_STUDY 14B-0 R7 and 14B item 4): the stove line, "Actually,
   New York does.", and "bro". Nobody on the panel had a "that's me" moment, and nobody learned a second fact. A
   pinned comment does not replace a beat in the film.
5. About 44 s in one golden place (car and platform). LESSONS 62 lists "too few locations" as a known gap.
6. Logic: at the first stop a silent finger works; at the second a silent finger fails. The film's claim is "a
   finger AND a shout". A sharp viewer (P4, P7) can poke at this.

### Fixes
1. Replace 0-4.8 s with B's opening, or at least put the tourist and the yellow suitcase sharp in frame 0.
2. Cut or rewrite "One is laughing. One is learning."; change "small finger" to "the boy's finger".
3. Give the lab a picture tie to the doorway (B's blinking door lamp before the cut, or a 1 s doorway insert
   between the six balls and the one ball).
4. Add "Same test." to the number line.
5. Buy back one viewer-facing beat: the stove line (4 s) or the New York reversal (two lines, about 4.5 s). Pay for
   it with "Vending machine... check!" plus the wordless thumbs-up (3.8 s) and one describing narrator line (3 s).
6. Board the three must-read beats as phone-size stills before the voice is locked.

**Shares: past 3 s 7/8, past 15 s 6/8, to the end 5/8.**

### Lines as spoken
- "This train driver points at a green light and shouts at it," Clear. But "This [person]..." is the stock opener of
  faceless Shorts, and it names the green light, so "pointing at nothing" (the title, and the tourist's line 3 s
  later) is untrue on screen.
- "Green!" in the comma: a delight when it lands. It needs a different voice dropped into a narrator sentence with
  0.08 s each side, and both halves of the sentence from one take, or the melody breaks.
- **"Yo! He's pointing at nothing!"** "Yo!" is a sitcom tic and a one-syllable shout under a laugh tag is a coin
  toss for an AI voice. Drop it.
- **"A finger and a shout drag it back."** After a pause and a cut, "it" is unclear (the mind? the bag?). Say "drag
  your mind back."
- **"Which is why..."** A narrator line after the payoff. It breaks "end on the last word" (YOUTUBE_STUDY 14A
  tactic 26), softens "Bag... gone.", and is a loop trick viewers now spot.
- "shouts at it" makes the driver sound silly for 3 s. It sits close to the rule that the joke is never on railway
  staff. The script's own swap ("and calls it out loud,") is safer.
- "The driver points at the speed." / "Then the doors.": with the two calls and the kid's echo this is list rhythm.

### Strengths
1. The most honest numbers check: it states its own floor case (9 / 14) and sets a recording gate.
2. The narrator names an act and the character does it one second later ("shouts at it," / "Green!").
3. The "why" is acted, not only replayed: the kid points at the real bag, the tourist looks right at them, gives a
   thumbs-up and looks back at his phone.
4. The tourist standing under the striped board in New York, never looking up, ties the detour to him for free.
5. The volunteer's yawn and wrong button shows "just looking" failing before the number is said.

### Weaknesses
1. No stake until 7-11 s; the opening leads with the famous fact and removes the title's mystery.
2. Narrator 117 of 153 words (76 %); 35.7-56 s is 20 s of narrator across four places.
3. Three driver calls and a kid echo in the first 19 s.
4. "Which is why..." after the payoff.

### Fixes
1. A person and a loss inside the first sentence, or use B's opening.
2. Do not name the green light in line 1.
3. Cut "Yo!", cut "Which is why...", say "drag your mind back."
4. One driver call before the bag plant, not three.

## 9. Must fix in the final script

1. **Open on the new angle.** The first picture and the first sentence are the tourist and his yellow suitcase, not
   a driver pointing. A plain, concrete loss is said or shown by 3 s. (A, C and D each lost the "seen it" viewer
   before 3 s.)
2. **One speaker for the first 3 seconds, and it is the narrator.** No unseen speaker, no overlapping voices. Any
   character who speaks in the first 5 s is on screen when he speaks.
3. **The hook reads with the sound off:** one voice's captions, the lost thing visible.
4. **Plan the hook's word count at the measured pace** (James about 5 syllables/s on running lines, slower on
   2-4 word sentences; a driver call is 1.3-1.5 s with its held beat). No clipped call-out. Add a recording gate.
5. **The suitcase is never idle for more than about 8 s in the first 30 s,** and no more than two driver call-outs
   come before it is planted in the doorway (visible alone for 2 s, by about 20 s, or by 28 s after a near miss).
6. **Never leave the tourist for more than about 6 s without a picture tie back to the doorway.** New York is 6 s
   or less, or he is in it. The lab is 8 s or less and is tied by a picture (the blinking lamp), not only by the
   melody.
7. **If frame 0 shows the outcome, the last 20 s must hold something frame 0 did not show** (the kid's two
   warnings, the stretched "Doors... closed!", "Bag... check!").
8. **Make the jump back in time readable** if B's opening is used: the same face, size and screen place on both
   sides of the cut, or B's one-shot fallback.
9. **Say the number in the clear form:** two short fragments plus "Same test.", with the jars showing 6 against 1.
   Not the 13-19 word single sentence in A, B and D.
10. **Remove the lines that sound written or unclear:** "One is laughing. One is learning."; "The man he's laughing
    at has over a thousand people behind him."; "For every six mistakes from just looking..."; "Pointing and saying
    it out loud turned every six mistakes..."; "Yo!"; "small finger"; "drag it back."
11. **End on "Bag... gone."** No narrator line after it.
12. **The "eyes looked right at it" beat must read at phone size:** stage it with the suitcase large and his eyes to
    the lens, not only as a small replay on a phone in his hand.
13. **Keep one viewer-facing beat in the film itself** (the stove line, word for word, as YOUTUBE_STUDY 14B item 4
    asks) and, if the time allows, the two-line New York reversal. A pinned comment does not replace either.
14. **Keep the fair clue:** if anyone says "at nothing", the picture shows the small green signal within 5 s.
15. **Voice tests before the lock:** one-word lines ("Check!", "Green!"), any tagged James line ("He is not.",
    "It stays.") recorded untagged first, and "There's nothing there!" checked for clarity.
16. **Plan 68 s or less with a cut order.** Never stretch audio.
