# Script doctor critique: "Why Japanese Train Drivers Point at Nothing" (pointing, v1 -> v2)

Reviewer: script doctor, 2026-11-19. I read: `SCRIPT_v1.md`; `research_notes/ricksclips/SCRIPT_REPORT.md` (sections 0, 2,
3, 5); the Railroads, Pink Prison Cell and Instinct transcripts; `reports/Next stories shortlist 2026-11-16.md`; the
owner's dialogue and pacing notes; and the viewer-panel critique `SCRIPT_critique_panel.md`. Facts re-checked today
against Wikipedia "Pointing and calling" (quotes in section 5.3) and Wikipedia "E235 series".

Scores are my judgment, not data. My v2 scores are self-grades of my own script, so discount them.

---

## 0. Verdict

- **Good topic, strong first 0.3 s, but v1 is an explainer with a cameo, not a story.** The "why" is half answered by
  10 s ("over a thousand people behind him" = it's for safety). The middle 21 s (18.6-39.6) drop both characters for
  history and New York. The loop line is a yes/no question everyone answers "yes". The answer is the most repeated fact
  on this topic (85 %). Then 22 s follow it, against our own rule of 12-15 s.
- **The payoff isn't earned.** v1 never shows the suitcase going into the train. And the tourist loses it by slapstick
  luck, not because he failed to check, so the ending doesn't prove the idea.
- **One factual error and two overstatements.** (1) The Shinkansen has no lineside signals (the panel's catch; I agree).
  (2) "in the middle of tunnels": the source says "in the tunnel". (3) "few railways bother" reads as a jab; the source
  only says "not common outside of Asia".
- **The fix in one sentence:** make it the doubter's story. He laughs in second 1. We promise he'll be pointing by the
  end. He leaves his bag on the train to film the man he's mocking. The "why" (your eyes look while your mind is
  elsewhere) plays over him doing exactly that, and the last frame is his own finger pointing at his bag as it leaves.

## 3. Structure

- **Is the answer in the right place?** On paper v1's answer is at 65 %. In effect it comes three times early: at
  7-10 s ("a thousand people behind him" = safety), at 27-39 s (New York adopted it after doors opened in tunnels), and
  then as a number at 41.8 s. So the "answer" is just a figure for something the viewer already believes. v2 holds two
  questions the viewer *can't* answer from memory: "how will the laughing guy end up pointing?" (frame 3 to the last
  frame) and the doubter's "what's a finger gonna do?" (53 % to the answer at 69 %).
- **Time from answer to end.** v1: about 22 s (41.8 s to about 64 s). That breaks our 12-15 s rule, and anti-theft lost
  its biggest share of viewers in that kind of tail. v2: 13.2 s of voice after the answer on the checker's
  slow pace model (why: 2 sentences, then 3 two-word payoff calls). That's about 11 s at the real voice's pace, plus
  about 3 s of picture, so about 14 s. Inside the rule, but with no slack: never add a line after the answer.
- **Does every step feel like a choice or a setback?** v1's middle is reported history ("It started", "Later",
  "Then", "Now"); nobody decides anything. v2 chains them. Stakes, *so* he points (choice). He leaves his bag to film
  (choice, and the seed of the setback). He claims nobody does it (a claim). New York's doors open in tunnels
  (setback), *so* conductors must point (choice), no board, no doors (the rule). He doubles down (choice). The test,
  the result, the why. The doors close on his bag (setback).
- **Is the doubter used well?** v1: he appears at 10 s, vanishes for 30 s, and his loss is luck. v2: he's on screen at
  1 s and promised a reversal at 3 s; he plants his own bag at 19 s; he's proven wrong at 23.5 s ("Actually, New York
  does."); he doubles down at 34-36 s; and he loses because of the exact mistake the why describes (looking while his
  mind is elsewhere). That's the shape of the reference channel's 30M+ hits: a mocker, proven wrong, by a picture.
- **Is the payoff ironic and earned?** v2 plants the bag three times: on screen at 0-1 s, parked and spoken at 19 s
  ("Stay. Be right back."), and visible in the doorway behind him at 21-23 s and 34-37 s. It plants the "Doors...
  closed!" call and its picture (doors shutting) at 18 s, and the kid's copying at 0-1 s and 8-13 s. The ending pays all three, plus the frame-3
  promise. Everyone who points gets it right; the one man who laughed is the one who didn't check.
- **Does the loop to frame 0 work?** v1 jumps from the platform to the cab, and "him" doesn't match the opening sound.
  v2's last shot is the tourist's phone screen again (the same record dot as frame 0) with his own finger pointing at
  the leaving train, on "gone". It cuts straight into frame 0: the same phone screen, the glove pointing, "Signal...
  green!". The gesture, the screen and the "X... Y!" call rhythm all match. A smooth, rewatchable seam.

**The panel's line, "That laugh... is about to cost him."** I agree with the idea (doubter first, plus a promise), not
the line. It's a stock trailer-voice line that works on any video. "Give him a minute. He'll be pointing too." is
specific to this story, it's ironic (the mocker will copy the man he mocks), it's literally the last picture, and it
makes the viewer guess *how*, which a vague "cost" doesn't. Its weakness: it gives away *that* he'll point. The
surprise has to come from *why* (his bag), and the plants make that fair.

**The phone frame.** I agree with filming frame 0 through his phone, but the phone screen must fill the whole frame,
with the glove big and readable at thumbnail size. Don't put a small phone inside the frame. The pull-back reveals the
phone in his hand.

### 4.2 Title and cover

- **Keep "Why Japanese Train Drivers Point at Nothing."** With the fixed picture (the signal is a dot far down the line),
  "nothing" is literally what it looks like. It's the "Why [group] [odd act]" formula, has search demand, gives no
  answer and has no number.
- Backup: "Why Japan's Train Drivers Talk to Themselves" (true, less "seen it", same formula).
- **I wouldn't use the panel's "The Tourist Who Laughed at a Train Driver" as the main title.** It puts our *invented*
  character in the title of a true-story channel, which invites "fake" comments. At most, A/B test it as a second title.
- Cover: the glove pointing at a tiny green dot, the laughing tourist with his phone up and the yellow suitcase.
  Text: "POINTING AT... NOTHING?" (3 words). On Shorts, frame 0 matters more than the cover, and v2's frame 0 is already
  a strong thumbnail.
- Pinned comment (keep v1's): "Be honest: how many times have you gone back to check the stove?"
