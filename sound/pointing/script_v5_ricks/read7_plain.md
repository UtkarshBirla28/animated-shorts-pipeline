# Read 7: plain-English, non-native, one hearing

## Lines I did not get on first hearing (both versions)
- "Five press anyway." The video said *tap your foot*, and now it says *press*. Press what? "Anyway" is easy to miss.
- "point at the light, and say it." Say what? The colour? "Signal clear"?
- "The story goes," is an idiom. I lose half a second working it out.
- "He never knew about the eyes." Who is he, the boss or the driver? I worked it out late, so the joke landed late.
- "your hand stops waiting for your brain." It's a nice picture, but I had to think about it. Hard at speaking speed.
- "That's how the cereal ends up in the fridge." I only got it if the picture shows it. "Ends up" is a phrasal verb.
- "conductors" made me think of music. "A striped board": what board? Only the picture saves it.
- "dead sexy": "dead" sounds like someone died. I got the prank only roughly.

## Only in M
- "Japan's train drivers do it all day." Do WHAT? The last verb I heard was "presses". The shout after it saves the line, but only just.
- "the shouting rule": I never heard "shout" before this. We only heard "say it". What rule?
- "So why does it work?" Twenty seconds of steam-train story come before it, so "it" is lost. I had to rewind in my head to the lab.

## Only in N
- None of the M problems. "So Japan's train drivers point at their signals, and shout" names the action and the shout. "Didn't start as science" works well, because I had just heard the science.

## The story in two sentences
A Japanese railway test found that people who point at a light and say it out loud stop making the autopilot mistake that people who only watch make. That's why Japanese train drivers point and shout, a habit that may have started with a steam-train driver whose eyes were going bad. New York copied it, and pranksters made fun of it. Point: pointing and saying it out loud stops your hand acting before your brain does.

## Did it hang together?
In N, yes: test, why it works (cereal), drivers, where the rule came from, who copied it. In M, the cereal and the "why" come after the history, so they feel like a second story. New York is a fun ending in both, but it only shows the idea spread. It does not teach the point.

7. "He never knew about the eyes." -> "The boss never knew why."
