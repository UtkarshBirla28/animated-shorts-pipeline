`RICKS_NARRATION_STUDY.md` (this folder; 32 Ricks Clips transcripts measured) and `SIDE_BY_SIDE.md`.

**In one line:** the v4 story stays, and so does its order. The changes are in the telling: fewer slow 2-3 word tag
sentences, the story chained with "So / But", the lab told in the present tense, a page-turn line where readers
swiped, the year moved to the board, one explanation said once. It comes out about 4 s shorter at the same real

shout is one breath and sits in a narrator gap. Line ids are the v4 ids: a line that survives keeps its number, so the
built shots map. Line 6 stays cut (it is the first thing to add back if the voice comes in short, see the reserves).

## WHAT CHANGED, AND WHY (each tied to the study)

1. **Fewer slow tag sentences (9 -> 5 outside the "Go" run).** His median is 1 sentence of 4 words or fewer per
   video (max 4); v4 had 9. On James these are the slowest words in the film (2.0-2.4 w/s against 2.9-3.4 inside
   longer sentences: "Then, red." took 1.32 s for 2 words). Joined: line 4 "A hundred lights, then red." (one
   sentence, measured 1.94 s against 2.72 s for the two), line 5 "Twelve people just watch, but five press anyway."
   Kept on purpose, because they are his "Not really." / "Rail touching rail." beat and our laughs: "She did.",
   "Not one presses.", "Only green.", "Did you tap?", "Now try again."
2. **The story is chained by "So" and "But" (sentence starts, v4: 2 "So", 0 "But"; v5: 3 "So", 1 "But", plus
   "but five press anyway").** He starts sentences with "So" in 29 of 32 videos and with "But" in 25 of 32. Line 5 gets its setback "but five press anyway"; line 11
   "Which is why" becomes "So" (2 words, about 0.6 s shorter); line 13 opens on "But".
3. **Line 13 is now a page-turn line at the swipe point.** Four of five round-5 readers named the move from the lab
   to the steam train (about 35-45 s) as where they would leave ("it feels like the video ended and a second one
   began", story reader). His turn lines sit at 40-70 % and re-open the question ("Then nothing happens.", "So what's
   in there?", "But why?"). "And where did it come from?" asks it flatly; "But the shouting didn't start as science." turns
   against what we just heard (a lab proved it) and opens "then how?". The story reader proposed nearly these words.
   It is also safer on the facts than v4 (see the facts table, line 13).
4. **The lab is told in the present tense ("just watch", "press", "have to point", "presses").** He opens on one past
   sentence and then tells the action in the present in 15 of his 19 tales that open in the past tense. Here it
   reads as the viewer's game going on, live. The steam and New York stories stay in the past: they are told tales
   ("the story goes"). Test item: if readers find the switch odd, the v4 past forms are measured and fit the same time.
5. **No year in the voice ("In 1994," cut, about 0.8 s).** He says a year in 1 of 32 videos; our best film says none.
   The year stays on the lab board, where his dates live too ("1979" on the pink-cell calendar).
6. **"You see," at the why (line 9).** He uses it in 22 of 32 videos, often to turn to the reason.
   It tells a first-time listener "here comes why" for 0.5 s. First reserve if the voice is long.
7. **Line 7 says "have to" (the extra rule as a duty), which the ending pays back: "the conductors had to point."**
   His endings re-use a planted word (the stump, "perfect circle", "packed that tight"). 2 words and about 0.4 s
   shorter than "got one extra rule:".
8. **Line 15 loses "who shouted back".** The coal man's "Clear!" says it in the next breath. He never explains the
   same thing twice, and the binge reader asked for exactly this cut. About 1 s.
9. **Left alone because they are already his manner:** "Did you tap? She did." (his deadpan two-word answer), the
   cereal line (his one everyday comparison: "Ever blown on a bowl of soup?"), "His boss rode along, loved it, and
   made it a rule." (his three-verb run-on), "He never knew about the eyes." (the twist at 77 %, his answer point is
   about 72 %), the prank and "And the conductors had to point." (an ironic picture that lands on the rule), and line
   22 (our loop, which he does not have and which every reader said makes them replay).

What I did NOT change, and why: his biggest tools are a doubter character who mocks the hero, invented dialogue and
one held mystery with invented stakes. Our facts give no doubter's words we may voice, and "no conversation" is a
fixed limit; the trap played on the viewer is our honest stand-in (the viewer is the one who is sure). His pace (3.7
words a second) is not reachable on James without stretching the voice, which is banned; so v5 gets its speed from
dropping slow tags, not from more words.
