# Two versions of one narration (about 67 seconds each)

A narrator tells this over cartoon pictures in a vertical one-minute video. The two versions use the same facts and
almost the same sentences; the ORDER of the middle differs. Lines in [brackets] are short shouts by characters or a
note of what the picture does.

---

## Version M

Tap your foot on every green light. Only green.
Go. Go. Go. Go. Go. Go. Go! [the light is red]
Did you tap? She did.
Japan's railway lab set a trap like this. A hundred lights, then red.
Twelve people just watch. Five press anyway.
Twelve others have to point at the light, and say it.
Not one presses.
Japan's train drivers do it all day.
[a train driver points at a green signal and shouts: "Signal clear!"]
But the shouting rule didn't start as science. The story goes, a steam-train driver's eyes had gone bad.
[the steam-train driver shouts: "Signal clear!"]
So he shouted every signal to his coal man.
[the coal man looks at the signal and shouts back: "Clear!"]
His boss rode along, loved it, and made it a rule.
He never knew about the eyes.
So why does it work? You see, after a hundred times, your hand stops waiting for your brain.
That's how the cereal ends up in the fridge.
Pointing and saying it makes your hand wait.
New York copied the pointing: conductors must point at a striped board.
So two New Yorkers held up a sign: "Point here if you are dead sexy."
And the conductors had to point.
Now try again. This time, point, and say it.
