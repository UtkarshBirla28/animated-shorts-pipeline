# Read 6: story editor (spine)

## P: railroad gaps (65 s)
1. A mocked young engineer leaves gaps in his rails, and in the heat the "perfect" rival railroad buckles under a train. Question at 0 s: "He sawed gaps... every 39 feet" (why?).
2. Joins: race to gaps CAUSED ("whoever finishes first gets the whole country's business"). Boss to "But he won't close" CAUSED. Rival finishes first, everyone switches: CAUSED. "Then summer comes": NEXT (it's time, but time is the engine here). Gaps shrink to boss gloats: CAUSED. "Meanwhile" groaning rails: CAUSED setup. Gaps close to rival kicks: CAUSED.
3. No sag. The false win ("Even your gaps know you were wrong") is the best middle in the pack.
4. Life-or-death stakes the opening only hinted at, and it lets you work out the why yourself.
5. Story 9, grip 8.

## T: the tree (59 s)
1. The more a town makes a lone tree impossible to miss, the more people hit it, sheriff included, even after it is a stump. Question at 0 s.
2. "But somehow" CAUSED. "So the town nails a giant flashing arrow" CAUSED. "Yet the crashes double" CAUSED. Tow-truck guy: CAUSED (comic result). "Eventually, the sheriff": NEXT. Ax: CAUSED. Stump: CAUSED. "So how does this keep happening?" turns to the explanation.
3. A small dip at the 6-line lecture, but it calls back the arrow, so it holds.
4. "Even when it's just a stump" pays off the opening and the sheriff gag in one line. The easiest to retell.
5. Story 8, grip 9.

## U: petrified wood sign (83 s)
1. A warning about theft makes an honest woman want to steal, and a test proves the warning causes it. Question at 0-4 s ("wanted to steal").
2. Sign to whisper CAUSED. "But why" to Cialdini CAUSED (a link through her fiancé). Two signs to results CAUSED. Explanation CAUSED. "And the bait wood?" NEXT, but it is a gift.
3. Sags at "Like a teacher saying... The other half hears" (three lines explaining something we already got). Also long.
4. "Stolen twice": new, funny, ironic.
5. Story 8, grip 7.

## R: the clown (74 s)
1. People on phone calls miss a unicycling clown and walk past free money, and the viewer gets tested the same way. Question at 0 s ("Count").
2. Test to "You saw him, right?" CAUSED. Hyman needs a distraction to student CAUSED. Interviews to 1 in 4: CAUSED. "Here's the weird part: music" NEXT. "So Hyman tried something sneakier" NEXT dressed as "so".
3. Sags at the music to "sneakier" handoff.
4. "Did you see the money?" turns the test back on the viewer. A real new sting.
5. Story 7, grip 8.

## Q / S / V: pointing at signals (same film)
1. Pointing and saying it stops your hand acting on autopilot. Japanese drivers do it, it started with a driver hiding bad eyes, and New Yorkers pranked the copy. Question: "Did you tap?" at about 6 s (V, S), about 9 s (Q).
2. Joins (V): test to lab CAUSED. Lab to "hand stops waiting" CAUSED. "So Japan's train drivers point" CAUSED. "But the shouting didn't start as science" a turn, but backwards in time: NEXT. Eyes to boss's rule CAUSED. "New York copied the pointing" NEXT. Prank CAUSED (small). "Now try again" CAUSED (bookend).
3. The spine's question is answered by about 25 s ("makes your hand wait"). After that nothing is open, so the legend and the prank are trivia hung on the hook. Stakes shrink: a lab test, then a legend, then a joke. No danger, no cost.
4. "Now try again" is a good bookend but adds nothing new. You repeat the opening; P, T, U and R all end on something you didn't have.
5. V: story 5, grip 6. S: story 5, grip 6. Q: story 4, grip 5.

### The three versions
- **V is the strongest.** It opens cold on the test, keeps "Twelve people just watch, but five press anyway" as one caused line, and has the best hinge: "But the shouting didn't start as science."
- **S** has the same cold open but weaker joins. "Twelve people just watched. / Five pressed anyway." loses the "but". "And where did it come from?" is a soft question where V has a turn. "Which is why" is fine. "who shouted back" adds nothing.
- **Q** has the best sentence ("just to catch them on one red") and the worst opening for this story: it tells you a red is coming, then says "Try it." The trap is spoiled before it is sprung.
- In all three the film is three things stapled together: a test, a legend, a prank. "He never knew about the eyes" is a lovely twist that leads to nothing. It is followed by "New York copied".

### Gap to the best (P)
Big: about 4 points of story. P plants one question and holds it to the last line, each beat raises the cost, and the ending is new. V answers its own question a third of the way in, then coasts.

### The one structural change
**Hold back the "why" until after the legend.** Keep the test and the result ("Not one presses"), then go straight to the drivers and the bad-eyes story. Then let the lab explain it: "your hand stops waiting for your brain... pointing makes it wait." Then do the prank tag and "Now try again". This turns "He never knew about the eyes" from a dead end into a setup that science pays off: a half-blind driver's cover story turned out to be right. It also keeps one question ("why did the pointers not press?") open until about 45 s instead of about 25 s. No new facts, same length.
