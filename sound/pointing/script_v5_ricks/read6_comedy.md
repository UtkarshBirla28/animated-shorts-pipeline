## Q / S / V: one film, three cuts. Post V.
Where they differ, line by line:
- Opening. S and V open cold with the game ("Tap your foot on every green light. Only green."), so the viewer plays before knowing it's a trap. Q opens by explaining the trap, then says "Try it." That spoils the "Go!" on red. "Only green." is the set-up that makes the red sting, and Q drops it. The S/V opening is strongest, and V's follow-up beat is tighter.
- The lab line. S: "In 1994, a railway lab in Japan set a trap like this. / A hundred lights. / Then, red." Three beats re-run a gag we just saw. V: "Japan's railway lab set a trap like this. / A hundred lights, then red." One clean beat. V wins.
- The results. Q and V: "Twelve people just watch, but five press anyway." S: "Twelve people just watched. / Five pressed anyway." S's split gives "five" its own beat, so S is funnier here. S's "got one extra rule: point at the light and say it" is clearer than "have to point at the light, and say it."
- "You see, after a hundred times" (Q/V) against "After a hundred times" (S). S is cleaner, because "You see" is throat-clearing.
- "So Japan's train drivers" (Q/V) against "Which is why" (S). "So" is quicker.
- The turn. Q and V: "But the shouting didn't start as science." S: "And where did it come from?" Q/V is far better: it sets up an expectation that "He never knew about the eyes" then punctures.
- The coal man. S's "who shouted back" says what the audio is about to do. Dead weight.

V has the right opening and the right turn. It only lacks S's split results line.

## Rewrites for V (no added length, no new facts)
1. "Twelve people just watch, but five press anyway." becomes "Twelve people just watch. Five press anyway."
2. "You see, after a hundred times, your hand stops waiting for your brain." becomes "After a hundred times, your hand stops waiting for your brain."
3. "Now try again. This time, point, and say it." becomes "Now try again. Point, and say it. Dead sexy." This is a callback button. The viewer pointing at the screen becomes the conductors pointing at the sign, so the ending gets a laugh instead of an instruction.
