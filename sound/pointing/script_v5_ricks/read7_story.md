## Where it sags
- **M:** "So why does it work? You see," "You see" is filler right on the payoff. New York after the payoff, around 54 s.
- **N:** "Pointing and saying it makes your hand wait. So Japan's train drivers point at their signals, and shout." This is a lecture line at 25 s, and the main question is already spent. After that, the eyes story has to carry 20 s alone. Then New York lands on a story that has already ended.

## The questions asked
- **Eyes story before or after the why:** before (M). It holds the "why" open across the middle of the video, the stretch a viewer is most likely to swipe away. In N, "But it didn't start as science" is a good second hook, but it is a smaller question than the one N has already used up.
- **"He never knew about the eyes." lands harder in M.** In N it is a punchline followed by a hard cut. In M the twist turns straight into a question: a rule born from a hidden weakness, "So why does it work?" That is the best join in either script.
- **The cereal line is funnier in N.** It comes 3 s after the viewer's own tap, so they recognise themselves in it. In M it comes about 25 s later, the tap memory is cold, and "You see," dulls it. M loses a little here.
- **New York:** stapled on in both. In M it is slightly better attached, because "copied the pointing" follows the mechanism just explained. In N it steps on the eyes punchline.
- **Last line:** it works in both. It calls back to the hook, gives a clean loop and asks for action. It is a touch stronger in M, because the mechanism is fresher.

## Choice: M
- **M:** story 7, grip 7
- **N:** story 6, grip 6
- **Difference: small.** It is not a coin toss. M keeps its main question open about 20 s longer and gets the best twist join. N only wins on the cereal line.

## Weakest sentence in M
> New York copied the pointing: conductors must point at a striped board.

This is the one NEXT join in the back half, at the point where nothing is open, so it is where viewers swipe. The replacement is shorter (12 words, down from 13), has no new fact, and is CAUSED by the line before it ("makes your hand wait"):

> So New York copied it: conductors must point at a striped board.
