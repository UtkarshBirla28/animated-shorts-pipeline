# Read 6: plain listener (English is not my first language, I hear it once)

## P (rail gaps)
- "his face goes purple": an idiom, but I guessed it means angry.
- "His gaps start shrinking": why? Nobody tells me that heat makes metal grow. I have to work it out myself, and many viewers will not.
- "pushing against each other like they want to get out": nice, but I understood it only halfway.
- "kicks sideways like a snake": did the train crash? It ends before I know.
- Story: a young engineer leaves gaps between rails and everyone laughs at him, but in summer the rails grow, his gaps close, and the "perfect" railroad bends. Point: leave room for things to grow (heat expands metal), but the script never says it.
- Clarity 6, interest 8. Most lost: "His gaps start shrinking." Understood most: the perfect railroad kicking sideways.

## Q (pointing, version 1)
- "flashed a hundred lights at people, just to catch them on one red": a fast first line; I don't know yet what the game is.
- "Tap your foot on every green light": there is no "only green" here, so I am not sure if red counts.
- "She did.": who is she?
- "Twelve people just watch, but five press anyway": press what? I was told to tap my foot. And watch what? There was no setup sentence like "Japan's lab did this test".
- "Twelve others have to point at the light, and say it": say what?
- "That's how the cereal ends up in the fridge": I get the joke slowly. It is not a common picture in India.
- "But the shouting didn't start as science": an abstract sentence, and hard on first hearing.
- "conductors must point at this board" / "dead sexy": I need the picture, and "dead sexy" is slang.
- Story: pointing at a light and saying it out loud stops mistakes made on autopilot, so Japan's drivers do it. It started with a half-blind driver, and New York copied it. Point: point and say it.
- Clarity 5, interest 6. Most lost: "Twelve people just watch, but five press anyway." Understood most: "your hand stops waiting for your brain."

## R (clown on a phone)
- "Count the people on a phone": for a second I heard people standing ON a phone.
- "Even asked straight out": an idiom, and a bit unclear.
- "Many spun around to look": who did? The phone people, after being asked? Unclear.
- "Nearly everyone ducked under it. On a phone? Only 6 in 100 stopped for the money": I lose the comparison. How many people not on a phone took the money?
- "Did you see the money?": only works if the money was in the opening scene. Otherwise I feel cheated.
- Story: people on phone calls miss a clown on a unicycle and even money hanging in front of them. Point: a call pulls your mind away.
- Clarity 6, interest 7. Most lost: the money part. Understood most: "a call pulls your mind toward someone who isn't there."

## S (pointing, version 2)
- "She did.": still I don't know who she is.
- "And where did it come from?": "it" is a little loose, but easy.
- "dead sexy" and "this board": the same as Q.
- Everything else is clear: "In 1994, a railway lab in Japan set a trap like this. A hundred lights. Then, red." Now I know the lab did my game. "got one extra rule: point at the light and say it." "who shouted back" helps me understand the coal man's line.
- Story and point: the same as Q, and I could say both easily.
- Clarity 8, interest 7. Most lost: "She did." Understood most: the lab sentences right after the game.

## T (tree on an empty road)
- "cruiser": a police car? I guessed it.
- "lawn chair", "tow truck guy": OK with the picture.
- Story: a lone tree on a straight road keeps getting hit. A warning arrow makes it worse, and the sheriff hits it and even hits the stump. Point: target fixation, meaning you steer toward what you stare at.
- Clarity 9, interest 8. Most lost: nowhere really. Understood most: "their hands slowly turn the wheel toward it."

## U (Petrified Forest sign)
- "Petrified Forest": I don't know "petrified" means stone wood. "Pieces" of what?
- Then "marked wood": I thought they were stones, so now I'm confused.
- "Her fiancé's professor, psychologist Robert Cialdini": too many links in one breath.
- "conscience pile": a hard word, though it is explained after.
- Story: a sign saying "many people steal" made more people steal. Point: we copy what others do, not the rules.
- Clarity 7, interest 7. Most lost: "Her fiancé's professor..." Understood most: "we don't copy rules. We copy each other." and the teacher example.

## V (pointing, version 3)

- It still has the problems from Q: "She did.", "just watch, but five press anyway" (press what?), "the shouting didn't start as science", "dead sexy".
- Clarity 7, interest 6.

## The three pointing versions (Q, S, V)
- Easiest: **S**.
- The game: in S and V it is clear ("Only green."). In Q it is less clear: there is no "only green", and the opening fact comes before I know the game, so it doesn't stick.
- Sentences that differ:
  - S "In 1994, a railway lab in Japan set a trap like this. A hundred lights. Then, red." beats V "Japan's railway lab set a trap like this. A hundred lights, then red." V is OK. Q has no bridge at all.
  - S "Twelve people just watched. Five pressed anyway." (short, past tense) beats Q/V "Twelve people just watch, but five press anyway." (present tense, which sounds like it is happening now).
  - S "got one extra rule: point at the light and say it" beats "have to point at the light, and say it".
  - S "And where did it come from?" beats "But the shouting didn't start as science."
  - S "to his coal man, who shouted back" beats "to his coal man." (Without "who shouted back" the coal man's "Clear!" comes from nowhere.)
- Do they fit together? Drivers: yes, clear in all three. Steam train: clear as "where it began" in S. In Q/V the "science" line makes me pause. New York: in all three it feels like a joke added at the end. I know they copied pointing, but "this board" needs the picture, and I'm not sure why the sign is the point. It's funny, but it doesn't connect back to the lights test.
- "Press" vs "tap your foot" is a mismatch in all three. Use one word.

## Must be made clearer
- All pointing versions: "She did." (who?), "press" vs "tap", "say it" (say what? "say the colour"?), "dead sexy" (slang), "this board".
- Q/V: "But the shouting didn't start as science."
- P: say once that heat makes the rails grow.
- R: "Many spun around to look"; the money comparison numbers.
- U: "Petrified Forest" (say "stone wood"), "Her fiancé's professor, psychologist Robert Cialdini".
