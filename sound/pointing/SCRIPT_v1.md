- **The first frame is strange and moving:** a man alone in a cab snaps his arm out and SHOUTS at nothing. Audio + motion
  in the first 0.3 s. The reason is hidden.
- **Proven demand, not yet done as an animated Short:** the long explainer "Why do Japanese train drivers point at
  everything?" has 2.8M views; no big animated Short exists. Japan + trains are two of the reference channel's biggest

## Facts (true; spoken claims only; everything else is cartoon staging)
| # | spoken claim | source |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Japanese train drivers point at signals / indicators and call out their status | Wikipedia "Pointing and calling" (shisa kanko), citing The Japan Times (21 Oct 2008, "JR gestures") |
| F2 | It began in the early 1900s on steam trains: driver and fireman ("boilermen") called out signals to each other; pointing was added decades later | Wikipedia, History section (cites Japan Times) |
| F3 | "Not common outside of Asia" (used in New York, Toronto, some Chinese-standard systems) | Wikipedia |
| F4 | New York: conductors point at a black-and-white striped board at every stop; introduced in 1996 (September 1996) after a series of incidents where doors opened in the tunnel; if the conductor cannot see the board, doors may not be opened | Wikipedia (cites MTA, 12 Nov 2013); Mental Floss "What are subway conductors always pointing at?"; Nowiknow "Safety Zebras" |
| F5 | A 1994 Railway Technical Research Institute test: on a simple task, pointing + calling cut mistakes by almost 85 % (2.38 to 0.38 per 100 actions) | The Japan Times 2008 via Wikipedia; EMBS Pulse ("Finger Pointing and Calling") repeats 2.38 and ~85 % |
| F6 | Why: it "helps with focus" | Wikipedia; Shinohara et al. 2013 (task-switching study) listed there |
| F7 | A bullet train carries over a thousand people (N700 series: 1,323 seats) | JR Central fleet data (common knowledge; say "over a thousand") |
Not claimed: who invented it, that foreigners find it silly, any New York error statistic (unverified), that NY conductors
do or do not call out. The tourist and the suitcase are a cartoon gag (fiction, labelled "dramatized" in the description).
