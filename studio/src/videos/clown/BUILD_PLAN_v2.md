## The ten laws of this rebuild (every owner, every shot)
1. THE CAMERA ALWAYS MOVES. Every frame of the film has camera motion: follow moves that ride WITH the action (dolly
   beside the clown, track a walker), slow pushes / pulls (creep 0.2-0.4 % per frame on holds), symmetric bell glides of
   30-55 f between framings inside a shot, gentle arcs. Never a locked frame. Never a jerk: velocity is continuous.
2. DEPTH AND PARALLAX in every shot: a near layer (leaves, a lamp post, a passing shoulder, a bike wheel) sliding
   past the lens, the subject in the middle, life behind. Lens low (chest or wheel height) for energy.
3. LIFE in every shot: at least three moving things besides the focal action (walkers crossing at different depths,
   pigeons hopping and flushing, a frisbee, a skateboarder, a cyclist, banners and leaves in the wind, petals, the
   fountain, a dog on a lead, students on the steps chatting with hand gestures). Background people are never frozen.
4. A NEW FRAMING ABOUT EVERY 2.5 S (~26 framings in ~66 s): a cut, a dissolve (6-8 f) or a big camera move that ends
   on a new composition. Hard cuts only on comic snaps (the clown suit reveal, the spin, the rewind).
5. EVERY CLAUSE IS SHOWN ON ITS WORD, and every story verb is PERFORMED and readable on a phone: rode, told, honked,
   asked, laughed, pointed, spun, ducked, clipped, hung, stopped, walked on.
6. ACTING: the gesture recipe (wind-up 4-6 f, move 5-7 f, overshoot 10-16 %, settle 8-10 f, hair / backpack / hat /
   horn 2-3 f late), expressions held >= 12 f, eyes open and alive (blinks, eye darts toward the action), walks with
   weight (bob 4-6 % of height, lowest 3-4 f after contact, 3-5 degree lean), moving holds (breath, weight shift),
   gesture hands 40-50 px at 1080 with clear shapes (point, thumbs-up, open-hand wave from the elbow, peg pinch).
   Characters who SPEAK a line move their mouths (talk cycle on the line's syllables) and gesture; the narrator never
   moves anyone's mouth. Phone people's mouths move (they are talking on calls).
7. SIZE: the focal character fills 35-60 % of the frame height (wides excepted); faces above the caption band
   (y 1280-1440 at 1080x1920); key things inside x 60-888, y 288-1248 where possible; no bare-brick lower halves:
   the lens is low and the floor carries shadows, pigeons, petals, a passing near walker.
8. COLOUR: the characters and the clown lead every frame (brick calmer and redder, see the LOOK owner); each shot has a
   colour idea (day square, warm office, the warm call bubble against a drained world, the cool slow-motion duck, the
   tape-rewind look at the end).
9. IN-WORLD BOARDS carry numbers and jokes (clipboard pages, an A-frame chalkboard, the office whiteboard), <= 6 words,
   caps >= 40 px at 1080, held >= 1.5 s. On-screen graphics only for the quiz chip, the 1-5 count pops and the ring.
10. HONESTY of the quiz: exactly five phones, all countable at phone size; the money visible and never pointed at
   until the end; the end replay shows what really happened in the quiz shot.
