## 4. The 5 weakest seconds

1. **33.7-36.4 s (f1011-1093).** 2.78 s with no voice, bed -26 dBFS average and -38 dBFS at 35.5 s, over a side-on boy whose point reads as fidgeting and then a man standing still. The single most likely retention dip in the film.
2. **38.5-42.4 s (f1150-1271, s16a).** 4 s of the narrator explaining over the film's least-alive shot (gate life 0.10), eyes shut, phone at the ear; the words say "his eyes looked right at it". This is where "I know this topic" viewers leave.
3. **5.5-7.0 s (f164-211, s03b).** The first beat after the hook: a driver facing us, a white blob fist at the lens, no signal visible, a -29 dBFS held beat. First post-hook dip risk; it also hides the film's own fair clue (he IS pointing at something).
4. **31.5-34.0 s (f943-1019, s13).** "VENDING MACHINE... CHECK!" with no vending machine in frame; a black faceless figure wipes across. A gag that should get a laugh gets a frown.
5. **20.4-22.0 s (f612-660).** 1.6 s near-silence after "BAD SUITCASE." while a stranger walks in; nothing new happens.
(Runner-up: 45.5-49 s, the lab: small jars at the top, bare table filling the lower half.)

## 6. Where a first-time viewer cannot tell where we are or who speaks

- 4.5 s (f135) and 38.0 s (f1140): his profile view has a dark goatee/sideburn shape and looks like a different man.
- 5.5 s (f164): cut to a driver facing us in a seat: no windscreen, no track; "where is this?" for a second.
- 21.6-23.4 s (f650-700) and 57-59 s (f1710-1770): "DOORS... CLOSED!" in CAPS while the only visible face is the salaryman (plant) or the laughing tourist (payoff). The driver is never on screen for these lines, so many viewers attribute the line to whoever is in frame.
- 28.0 s (f840): the turning card shows a mirrored ghost face; then he vanishes behind the door leaf instead of stepping out to the platform.
- 42.5-45 s (f1272-1350): the phone replay of "WHAT'S A FINGER GONNA DO?" while he holds the phone at his ear with an open mouth: him, or a caller?
- 49 s (f1467) and 56 s (f1675): one-second jumps (lab to platform to lab; platform to cab) are fine with sound, abrupt muted.

## 8. Ending and loop

The payoff lands: "DOORS... CLOSED!" on the exact plant framing, then the boy's "BAG... CHECK!" turning his head, then "bag... gone." is the right order and the right size. The seam is continuous (gates: 23.7 vs limit 37.6); the last shot is frame 0's camera; "His bag is on that train. He is not." reads as a tag joke on the second pass. It earns a partial second view (2-3 s) from maybe a third of finishers. The one thing spoiling it: the face-crossing single-finger hand at f1898-1907, the last image before the loop.

## 9. Unique vs generic

Unique (keep): the doubter who mocks the gesture and loses his bag to the exact failure it prevents; the boy who copies the driver and ends up saving/warning him; the call-out form used as a comic motif ("VENDING MACHINE... CHECK!", "BAG... CHECK!", "bag... gone."); the outcome-first hook; the loop where he finally points properly, too late. No other pointing-and-calling video I know of tells it as a story with a bag.
Generic: the title wording (the same phrasing as many explainers); "1,000 people behind him"; the 6-vs-1 lab number (the familiar ~85 % stat in other clothes); the narrator's "You see, ..." explanation block. The topic itself is a well-worn "Japan is different" subject: viewers who have seen it leave at the explanation, not at the story.

## 10. Changes that would most raise average percentage viewed (most valuable first)

1. **Close the 2.78 s gap (f1011-1093).** Cut s14 to about 35 frames and start s15 so "THIS KID GETS IT!" begins about 1.2 s earlier, or put the boy's audible "fwip" plus a short tense pluck at 34.2 s and lift the bed to about -20 dBFS. Target: no voice gap over 1.2 s and no half-second under -28 dBFS. (+2-3 pts)
2. **Make the vending-machine gag visible (s13, f943-1019).** Reframe so the machine is screen-left in frame and his arm points at it on "VENDING MACHINE...", or insert a 10-12 frame close of the machine on "CHECK!". Keep the doorway with the bag behind him. (+1-2 pts, plus comments)
3. **Bring s16a alive and true to the words (f1150-1356).** Phone held in front of his face with the lit screen toward us (his own clip on it), his eyes on it; on "looked right at it" pull focus to (or push past him onto) the bag and the boy; for the replay keep the phone in front with his own face on its screen so the "phone speaks" read is obvious. Or trim 0.5-1 s of orbit. (+2 pts)
4. **Show the boy's finger causing the save (s08, f509-560).** Reframe/stage so the boy and his full arm are in frame from f520, the tourist's eyes follow the arm, then he lunges. This is the film's fair clue. (+1 pt, and muted clarity)
5. **Fix the last-beat hand (f1896-1908).** Keep the draw-back hand beside the ear, never across the face, or turn it palm-forward with the finger relaxed. (Protects the loop and the comment section.)
6. **s03b (f164-211).** Keep the over-the-shoulder s03 camera through "SIGNAL... GREEN!" (glove next to the green lamp, then the snap), or put the lamp in s03b's frame; drop the fist-at-lens foreshortening. (+1 pt: the first post-hook seconds)
7. **s05 (f262-343).** Fill the car: standing commuters at the straps and in the aisle so "1,000 people" looks true.
8. **Lab (s17/s19, f1356-1600).** Push in so the jars fill about 60 % of the height and the empty table leaves the lower half; show the volunteer's face beside the jars.
9. **Say who speaks "DOORS... CLOSED!".** A small cab/speaker glyph before the caption (like the phone glyph), or a 6-frame cab insert on "DOORS...".
