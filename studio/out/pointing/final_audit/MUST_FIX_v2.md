# MUST-FIX list for v2 of the pointing Short (from three independent audits of the FINAL v1, 2026-11-20 05:50)

The owner watched FINAL v1 and said "it looks very bad". The independent audits agree it is not ready:

- **HOOK FORENSICS**: score 78 / 100 (hook 7.5, holds 7, look 7); predicted stay past 3 s 76 %, average viewed 80 %. POLISH THEN UPLOAD: the opening already passes every measured pattern of the 24 hits, but the suitcase is gone by 0.6 s, the frame-0 face reads 'hm?' not loss, the driver's point reads as a white blob and a wave, and the seam pose reads as a middle finger. These are a few hours of CAST and camera work, need no new voice, and bring the hook to about its ceiling for this story (about 78-82% stay past 3 s).
- **A panel of 8 real scrolling viewers**: score 72 / 100 (hook 7.5, holds 6.5, look 7); predicted stay past 3 s 75 %, average viewed 74 %. POLISH THEN UPLOAD: the hook and the payoff are strong and the story mostly works with the sound off, but a 2.8 s dead stretch at 34 s, a vending-machine gag with no machine in frame, a lifeless 4 s lecture shot whose picture contradicts its words, an unseen key save beat and a rude-looking last hand gesture each cost retention, and all can be fixed with edits and restaging rather than a new script.
- **CRAFT, LOOK AND DEFECTS, every shot**: score 62 / 100 (hook 6.5, holds 6.5, look 5.5); predicted stay past 3 s 70 %, average viewed 72 %. POLISH THEN UPLOAD: the story, hook timing and loop work, but a normal viewer will see the middle-finger-like hand at the loop point (s24 f1899-1907), the handless arm in the hook, the boy's payoff call hidden by the phone, and ghosted figures, and the dare's replay is inaudible.

Full reviews: audit_hook.md, audit_panel.md, audit_craft.md in this folder. The LOOK (colour, light, framing, the main
character's outfit) is handled separately: studio/out/pointing/look_test/ (DIAGNOSIS.md, LOOK_PLAN.md).

## CAST

- **[high] s16a + s16b** (frames 1150-1356 (38.3-45.2 s); from the A pan audit)
  - Problem: 4 s of narration over the least-alive shot in the film (gate life 0.10). The words say 'his eyes looked right at it' while his eyes are shut and he faces the camera. He holds the phone at his ear like a call, so the replay of 'WHAT'S A FINGER GONNA DO?' reads as him talking again or as someone on a call. Viewers who already know the topic leave here.
  - Fix: Have him hold the phone in front of his face, screen lit toward us with his own clip on it, eyes on the screen. On 'looked right at it', pull focus or push past him onto the bag and the boy in the doorway. For the replay, keep the phone in front with his face on its screen. Trim 0.5-1 s of the orbit.
- **[high] s24** (frames 1899-1907; from the CRAFT audit)
  - Problem: Between the 'hand by the ear' pose and the forward snap, his hand passes in front of his face with one finger up and the back of the hand to camera. At phone size it reads as a middle finger at the lens, and at f1900 the finger pokes his eye. These are the last frames before the loop goes back to frame 0.
  - Fix: Keep the ear pose outside the head outline (hand at least 60 px right of the head) and send the arm from the ear to full stretch forward and out to screen-left, below chin level, never across the face. Use the pointing-hand card with the thumb visible and the palm three-quarter on for the whole path.
- **[high] s01** (frames 20-50; from the CRAFT audit)
  - Problem: Inside the hook, the suitcase leaves the frame at f20 (0.67 s; the script says 1.4 s). From f20 to f50 the pointing arm is a stretched plank with no hand (flat cut end at x of about 88), aimed at a blank train wall.
  - Fix: Lock the forearm to the rig length so the hand card stays attached. Slow the door window so the suitcase stays under his fingertip until about f42.
- **[medium] s03b driver close-up** (frames 164-172, 173-190, 191-212; from the HOOK  audit)
  - Problem: The driver's point is aimed straight at the lens, so the index finger foreshortens into a ball on a white mitten. At phone size it is a white blob, not a pointing finger, and the film's central gesture is unreadable in its first clear view. The 'hand by the ear' beat is an open palm facing the camera with a big open grin (f173-180), so it reads as the driver waving hello. Half-lid smug blinks at f207-208 and f211 make the eyes flicker.
  - Fix: Rotate the point 35-45 degrees off the lens axis, up and to screen-left toward where the lamp would be, so the index finger reads in profile. On the held beat use a closed hand by the ear with the index finger still out, not an open palm. Keep the mouth firm (closed or a small flat shape) instead of the grin on the beat. Remove the half-lid frames f207-208 and f211.
- **[medium] s24 (last shot, into the loop)** (frames 1900-1907 (63.33-63.57 s); from the HOOK  audit)
  - Problem: On the 'Bag...' beat, his 'hand by the ear' pose puts the back of his hand in front of his nose with one upright finger. At phone size it reads as a middle finger or nose-picking, on the most re-watched second of the film, right before the loop.
  - Fix: Put the hand outside the head silhouette, beside the ear at cap height, with the index finger pointing forward toward the window, so no single upright finger crosses his face. Keep the timing of the sag (f1908-1911) and the lock-out into f0 unchanged.
- **[medium] s01 frame 0** (frames 0-20 (blink at 17-19); from the HOOK  audit)
  - Problem: The frame-0 face reads as 'hm?' (slightly knit brows, eyes down-left, small flat mouth), not as loss, and a half-lid blink at f17-19 makes the first second look bored. The hook research ranks a face with a clear emotion aimed at the hook object among the strongest stoppers. The point and gaze are on target; the emotion is not.
  - Fix: On f0-f20 use raised brows, wide eyes and a small 'o' mouth (the same 'o' card is already at f1908-1909, so the seam still matches), easing to the flat deadpan by f30 for 'He is not.'. Move the f17-19 blink to about f58.
- **[medium] s24 (last beat before the loop)** (frames 1896-1908 (63.2-63.6 s); from the A pan audit)
  - Problem: As he pulls his hand back to his ear, the back of the hand with one raised finger crosses his face. At phone size it reads as a middle finger or a nose-pick on the last image before the loop.
  - Fix: Route the draw-back so the hand stays beside the ear and never crosses the face. Or turn the palm toward the camera with the finger relaxed until the lock-out snap.
- **[medium] s20** (frames 1619-1673; from the CRAFT audit)
  - Problem: The boy is out of frame. His arm is a grey pole over 600 px long crossing the frame and the tourist's body, so it reads as a stick, not a child pointing at the bag.
  - Fix: Widen the shot by 10 %, or move the boy so his shoulder and cap brim are inside the right edge. Cap the arm at the rig length.

## DIRECTOR

- **[high] s01 (and s24 for the seam)** (frames 0-40 (the suitcase is gone by f16-20); from the HOOK  audit)
  - Problem: The hook object, the yellow suitcase, is on screen for only about 0.55 s. It whooshes out of frame left by f16-20 (yellow area 8.1% to 1.8% by f24), against the 1.4 s planned. From 0.67 s to 2.70 s the viewer sees a man pointing at a blank cream car side, so the loss is carried by the caption, not the picture. The cause is a tighter frame-0 framing than planned (head 27% of the frame height, not 19%) and about 45 px/f of window motion.
  - Fix: Pull the camera back so the head is about 21% at f0. Add a yaw track that follows the door window at about 70% of its screen speed from f0 to f30, easing out by f45, so the suitcase stays beside his fingertip to about f36-40. Keep the push to about 28% by f80 (the f51 punch-in can stay). Run the same rig on s24 at film time + L, with the track's ease-in in s24's last 6 frames, so the seam stays continuous. Switch the caption to 'on that train.' when the yellow touches the frame edge.
- **[high] s14 into s15** (frames 1011-1093 (33.66-36.44 s); from the A pan audit)
  - Problem: 2.78 s with no voice. The bed averages -26 dBFS and falls to -38 dBFS at 35.5 s. On screen the boy's point is side-on and reads as fidgeting, then a man stands still. This is the most likely retention dip in the film.
  - Fix: Cut s14 to about 35 frames and pull s15 earlier so 'THIS KID GETS IT!' starts about 1.2 s sooner. Alternatively, put the boy's audible 'fwip' and a short tense pluck at 34.2 s and lift the bed to about -20 dBFS. Target: no voice gap over 1.2 s and no half-second under -28 dBFS.
- **[high] s13** (frames 943-1019 (31.5-34.0 s); from the A pan audit)
  - Problem: 'VENDING MACHINE... CHECK!' plays with no vending machine anywhere in the frame. He points at the lens or off to the left, and a faceless black figure wipes across. The machine first appears at f1080 in s15. Viewers with the sound off miss the gag, and viewers with sound ask what he is pointing at.
  - Fix: Reframe s13 so the machine sits screen-left in frame and his arm lands on it on 'VENDING MACHINE...'. Keep the open doorway with the bag and the boy behind him. Or insert a 10-12 frame close-up of the machine on 'CHECK!'.
- **[high] s23** (frames 1815-1853; from the CRAFT audit)
  - Problem: The boy's payoff call 'Bag... check!' cannot be seen: the tourist's phone and hand cover the boy's face and pointing arm from f1815 to the cut.
  - Fix: Bring the tourist in 120-150 px further left, or put the phone in his screen-left hand, so the boy's face, point, hand to ear and snap are clear from f1810 to the cut.
- **[medium] s08** (frames 509-560 (17.0-18.7 s); from the A pan audit)
  - Problem: On 'the boy's finger saves it' the boy is outside the frame. His fist only shows at the far-left edge at f555. With the sound off, the save reads as the man grabbing his own bag, so the film's fair clue and the first finger payoff are lost.
  - Fix: Restage or reframe so the boy and his whole pointing arm are in frame from about f520. The tourist's eyes should visibly travel along the arm to the bag, then he lunges. Keep the wag on 'BAD SUITCASE' inside the frame, not cut off at the right edge.
- **[medium] s03b** (frames 164-211 (5.5-7.0 s); from the A pan audit)
  - Problem: This is the first beat after the hook. On 'SIGNAL... GREEN!' the driver faces the lens and the glove is a huge white foreshortened fist punching at the camera. No signal is in the frame. A -29 dBFS held beat sits at 6.0 s. It reads as 'he is pointing at me', hides the fact that he really is pointing at something, and gives no sense of place.
  - Fix: Hold the over-the-shoulder s03 camera through 'SIGNAL... GREEN!' with the glove beside the green lamp, then the snap. Or put the lamp in s03b's frame and swap the at-lens fist for a three-quarter point toward the windscreen.
- **[medium] s02 / s05 / s11** (frames 131-136; 300-312; 840-850; from the CRAFT audit)
  - Problem: On fast moves the tourist turns see-through or double-exposed. At f131 NEW YORK is doubled; f133 is a crossfade between the front and profile cards with the background showing through; f136 is a smear of several outlines; f300-312 the background shows through his torso as he passes the lens; f840-850 the door frame shows through his body on the hop out.
  - Fix: Swap character cards on a single frame with no crossfade. Turn off frame-accumulation blur on character cards, or apply only opaque silhouette motion blur.
- **[medium] s03 / s21** (frames 140-164; 1675-1706; from the CRAFT audit)
  - Problem: Both cab shots are dark (L* 38.5 and 38.0) next to neighbours at 51-60, so the film dips four times: -13, +14.5, -13.5 and +16.8 L* at the cuts. The lower half is a flat black console. The left third is the driver's out-of-focus back-of-head card, a brown blob. Out-of-focus dust specks look like dirt on the lens.
  - Fix: Lift the cab to L* about 50 (+0.6 EV) and warm it to b* about 16. Make the console warm dark-grey brushed metal lit by the windshield. Remove the dust particles in the cab shots. Sharpen or crop the driver's head card to a thin edge.

## SOUND

- **[medium] s16b (audio)** (frames 42.72-43.85 s; from the CRAFT audit)
