## 3. Can this core idea carry a fun minute?

**The topic: yes. This story: no.**

- **The topic has everything a hit needs:** a strange act you can picture (grown men in white gloves shouting at
  green lights and empty tracks), a counter-intuitive true result (about 6 mistakes become about 1, from the 1994
  Railway Technical Research Institute test, 2.38 % vs 0.38 %), a built-in doubter (anyone who sees it laughs), and a
  true ironic twist (New York's own subway conductors point at a striped board at every stop).
- **The v3 story wastes all of it:**
  1. **The bag is lost because of a silly choice, not because of the habit.** Parking a suitcase in an open train
     doorway to buy a drink is not a mistake viewers recognise from their own lives, so they don't feel "that's me".
     They think "idiot".
  2. **The hook uses up the ending.** Line 1 is the last picture.
  3. **The hero is never proven right in the plot.** The driver's pointing saves nothing on screen. The boy's "save"
     at 17-19 s cannot be seen.
  4. **The stake is tiny.** A suitcase, in the one country famous for returning lost things.
  5. **The jokes depend on tiny gestures read at phone size:** a foot hooking a wheel, a 0.7 s look, a boy's point
     at a bag 10 m away. The script itself admits it: "18 s is picture and sound only. Six things must read at phone
     size ... None has been drawn" (SCRIPT_v3 10.6, item 2).
- **Could the anti-theft shape (an experiment on ordinary people with one object, and a counter-intuitive result)
  carry it?** Partly. The real 1994 test was people pressing buttons at lamps. As a picture it is dull, and the
  source names no people. It works as the PROOF, not as the spine. The spine has to be **one ordinary person running
  the experiment on himself, by accident, with one object**: the tourist mocks the habit by copying it, the joke
  works, he quits, and the object leaves without him. That is an experiment (habit on / habit off) with one object
  (the yellow suitcase) and a counter-intuitive result (the silly mocking copy actually works). The rewrite in
  section 7 uses that shape.

## 4. Ranked: why the others are better (story lens)

1. **They end on something new.** Anti-theft: "stolen twice". Salad oil: "Warren Buffett". Clown: "Did you see the
   money?". Railroads: the buckle. Ours ends on line 1 repeated ("bag... gone." = "His bag is on that train.").
   The one new twist the topic had, New York, was cut into the pinned comment (SCRIPT_v3 lines 775, 800, 821).
2. **Cause and effect.** In every better film the strange act causes the result (the sign makes people steal; the
   gaps save the railroad; the arrow causes the crashes). Ours: the pointing has no link to the loss. He loses the
   bag by leaving it in a doorway (27.1-33.7 s).
3. **The first line is a strange act, not an ordinary outcome.** "He sawed gaps into a railroad every 39 feet" vs
   "His bag is on that train". The title's own promise (pointing at nothing) is never the opening picture, and the
   narrator is barred from saying it (fact guard 2).
4. **Real laughs that move the plot:** Instinct has 6 in 59 s, the Burglar 4 in 58 s, our anti-theft and salad oil
   2-3. Ours has about 1 ("bag... gone." at 62 s). Of its 8 joke tries, 5 depend on gestures that cannot be read at
   phone size.
5. **Something real is at stake, and it grows.** $150 million, a canyon railroad, a crash a week then double. Ours:
   one suitcase. The only big stake, "1,000 people behind him" (8.8 s), is never mentioned again.
6. **Density.** 213-246 words in the references, 182-213 in our earlier films, **135** in ours. 2.12 words/s, five
   voice-less gaps of 1 s or more (7.5 s), the longest 2.7 s at 33.7 s. About 22 s (34 %) where no story moves.
7. **The explanation is short, simple and comes after the payoff.** Ours: 17.8 s, said three times, a nested ratio
   sentence, and placed in the middle of the climax (the lab at 45.9-53 s cuts between leaving the bag and the
   doors closing).
8. **Variety of pictures.** Anti-theft has 7 places and salad oil 12, each with readable boards (14 TONS, 2 % / 8 %,
   90 % FULL, COUNTED: 3). Ours has 4 places, ~47 % of frames on the same tourist, ~62 % with the same yellow bag,
   and only one picture that is big or absurd: none.
9. **Real people talking (our own channel's strength).** "We'd better get ours now." "I own a unicycle... and a
   clown suit." "Ninety percent full!" Ours is a made-up tourist whose lines are dares ("WHAT'S A FINGER GONNA DO?",
   used twice: 24.1 s and 42.7 s).

## 5. Root causes in how this script was made

1. **A committee, scored by AI judges against a rule list, with no "is it fun?" test.** Four writers (A, B, C, D).
   The winner is a stitch: "Body = candidate C ... Hook and loop = candidate B" (SCRIPT_v3 header). Judges scored C
   at 77/100 and B at 72-75/100. The three final audits of the film said "POLISH THEN UPLOAD" and predicted 72-80 %
   average viewed (MUST_FIX_v2.md). The owner said 1/10. LESSONS 50 already says this happened before ("Six audits
   scored cut 1 at 4.7-6; the owner's verdict was harsher than any of them. Audit the IDEA ... before auditing the
   polish."). It was not applied.
2. **The script was optimised for its first 3 seconds.** The outcome-first hook won because the panel said "8 of 8
   past 3 s". That hook used up the ending. Nobody scored the last 10 seconds for surprise.
3. **Rules piled up until there was no room for comedy.** 7 fact guards and 8 culture guards: the tourist "is
   cheerful and never rude to staff", "never taps the glass", "Commuters step politely", "no music sting on the
   sleeping or the late salaryman", "The narrator never says the driver points at nothing". Plus style rules: "104
   words have one syllable, 27 have two, none has more" and "no emotion twice in a row". Every rule is fair on its
   own. Together they removed every absurd beat and gave choppy, flat lines ("First stop." "It stays.").
4. **Paperwork instead of story.** SCRIPT_v3.md is **113,977 bytes for 131 planned spoken words**: about 870 bytes per
   word. The anti-theft SCRIPT.md is **7,294 bytes for 217 words**: about 34 bytes per word. Sections 10.2-10.4 tick
   off "every judge must fix", "17 audit blockers" and "40 audit improvements". Not one section asks "where does the
   viewer laugh?"
5. **Rules cut the best material.** "No third story over about 4 s" (H7) and the lead's decision 1 cut the New York
   reversal and the stove "that's me" line. The script itself admits the result: "fewer 'that's me' moments than v2"
   and "weaker than v2" (10.6 item 3; line 779).
6. **The word budget was set too low, and the story was handed to animation.** 131 planned words (2.0 words/s),
   with "18 s ... picture and sound only" built on six tiny gestures that "none has been drawn". Our kit cannot
   reliably do a boy's side-on point or a foot hooking a wheel at phone size. The script needed 14 arm-out points,
   and the arms are exactly what the owner calls glitchy.
7. **A fable without fable logic.** Ricks Clips' fables are absurd on purpose and escalate. Ours is a quiet,
   realistic slice of life (a man, a bag, two stops) with one contrived choice at its centre. It is neither a true
   story (our strength: anti-theft, salad oil, clown) nor a wild fable (his strength).
8. **No side-by-side read against the films that worked.** Nobody put the transcript next to anti-theft's and
   Railroads' and asked which one you would keep watching. Lesson 50 asks for exactly this, but for picture only.

---

## 6. Can it be saved by edits?

No. No line or beat edit fixes the three broken parts: the hook spends the ending, the loss has no link to the
pointing, and nothing grows. Fixing those means a new spine, which is a new script. The world, cast and props built
for it CAN all be reused. So the answer is a rewrite of the story and voice, not a
new film from zero.

---

## 7. THE REWRITE: "Suitcase... check!" (beat sheet, every line)

**Title:** Why Japanese Train Drivers Point at Nothing (kept). Backup for A/B: "He Laughed at Japan's Train Drivers.
Then He Copied Them."

**Shape:** one ordinary man runs the experiment on himself, by accident, with one object. He mocks the habit by
copying it, the joke WORKS (the counter-intuitive result), he quits, and the object leaves without him. The real lab
proves why, in one sentence. The last line turns the joke on him: his own city does it too. The doubter is proven
wrong three times: the joke works, quitting costs him, and New York.

**Reuses:** the Tokyo platform (dressed as 3 stations by time of day and the station number pictogram), the commuter
car, the driver's cab, the small lab, the driver, the tourist (NEW YORK shirt), the boy and his mum, commuters, the
yellow suitcase. **New, small:** a coffee cup, a passport and a phone-screen card (a chat bubble with a cartoon NYC
conductor pointing at a black-and-white striped board). A second lab volunteer (a commuter card).

**Length:** 166 spoken words. Estimated 65.4 s at James's measured in-line speed (3.3 words/s) with 0.18 s joins
(the timing script is `benchmark/story/beat_times.py`). Honest range 63-70 s. Never time-stretch. If it runs over
68 s, cut in this order: (1) "He sent the clip home to New York." → keep it as a 1 s picture with no words (the
plant is then visual only), (2) "Just looking: six mistakes. Pointing and saying it out loud: about one." → a board

**Voices:** N = narrator (James, eleven_v4, the clown / salad oil voice the owner liked). T = tourist (loud, fast,
cocky, a real laugh in his mock lines; never a fake accent). D = driver (calm, crisp; name, held beat, status). K =
the boy (high, proud, the driver's rhythm). Every call-out keeps the real rhythm: point on the name, hand back beside
the ear, snap on the status.

never aimed at the lens, never crossing the face, and never longer than the rig. Gags on objects are close

## 9. Rules for every future film (story)

1. **The ten-sentence test, before any scoring.** Write the story as about 10 plain sentences and read it next to the
   anti-theft transcript and two reference transcripts. If ours is not as gripping and as funny read aloud, do not
   build it. AI judges' scores never approve a script; only this side-by-side read does.
2. **Line 1 is a strange act you can picture, with the reason hidden.** Never an ordinary outcome, and never the
   ending.
3. **The ending must show something the hook did not.** A new twist or fact in the last 10 s (stolen twice, Warren
   Buffett, did you see the money?, New York).
4. **The strange act must CAUSE the result.** The doubter is proven wrong by the hero's act itself, not by an
   unrelated mistake.
5. **The mistake at the centre must be one every viewer has made** (forgetting the one thing you meant to take), not
   a contrived choice.
6. **Something big or absurd at stake, and it grows** in 3-6 "So..." / "But..." steps.
7. **At least one new story event every 5 s, and at least 5 real laughs per minute,** each one also a plot step.
   Mark them in the script; a joke that needs a gesture under about 10 % of the frame height does not count.
8. **170-210 spoken words for 60-70 s.** No voice-less gap over 1.0 s except one planned beat. No more than 6 s of
   narration over a picture where nothing changes.
9. **The why is 1-3 short sentences, said once, after the answer event.** Numbers as two parallel short sentences
   ("Just looking: six. Point and shout: about one."), never a nested ratio.
10. **The best surprising fact stays IN the film.** Never move it to the pinned comment to obey a "no third story"
    rule.
11. **One script file of 10 KB or less** (the voiced script, the beat sheet, the facts). Guards live in one shared
    file and may not remove a joke without the lead naming the joke they will replace it with.
12. **At least 4 places or looks, no subject in more than 40 % of frames,** and a new framing every 2-3 s.
13. **Write for what the kit can animate.** Every gesture gag has its target in the same frame and is seen side-on.
    Tiny gestures (a foot hook, a 0.7 s look) never carry a story beat.
14. **Prefer true stories with real people's own words** (our three best films). When we use fiction, it must be
    absurd and escalate like a fable, not a quiet slice of life.
